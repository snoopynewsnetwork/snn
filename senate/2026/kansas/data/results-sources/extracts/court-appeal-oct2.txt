       This appeal concerns K.S.A. 25-1132, which defines the method and timeframe in
Kansas for submitting advance-voting ballots, commonly known as mail-in ballots. For
about 40 years, K.S.A. 25-1132 required that advance-voting ballots must be received by
a county election office by Election Day to be counted. In 2017, the legislature amended
the statute, requiring election offices to count advance-voting ballots postmarked before
the close of the polls on Election Day so long as they were received within three days
after the election. In 2025, the legislature eliminated that three-day window, setting the

The standards governing our decision

       K.S.A. 60-262(f) recognizes an appellate court's authority to "stay proceedings, or
to suspend, modify, restore or grant an injunction, while an appeal is pending," as well as
its authority "to issue an order to preserve the status quo or the effectiveness of the
judgment to be entered." Requests for stays at the appellate level are relatively rare, and
Kansas courts have not previously identified a distinct set of factors that address when a
stay on appeal is warranted.

       But a stay and an injunction are not the same remedy. As Nken explained, "a stay
operates upon the judicial proceeding itself." 556 U.S. at 428. The relative burdens of
persuasion of the parties seeking relief are also different. The plaintiffs—the parties
bringing the suit—bore the burden of establishing their entitlement to the temporary
injunction in the district court. The Secretary—the party seeking relief on appeal—now
bears the burden of establishing why that injunction should be suspended during appellate
review. But the equitable considerations underlying the two inquiries substantially
overlap. See 556 U.S. at 434. We therefore find Nken's four factors provide useful
persuasive guidance in exercising our authority under K.S.A. 60-262(f).

       The election calendar magnifies the importance of continuity while this appeal
proceeds. Kansas law generally understands the status quo for purposes of temporary
relief as "the last actual, peaceable, noncontested position of the parties which preceded


                                              6
the pending controversy." U.S.D. No. 503 v. McKinney, 236 Kan. 224, 227, 689 P.2d 860
(1984). In its order denying the Secretary's previous requests in this case, the Kansas
Supreme Court found the district court's conclusion reasonable that this baseline was the
law applicable when this case was filed—the three-day window that predated the 2026
amendments to K.S.A. 25-1132. Kansas Appleseed, No. 130,839, Order at 5. Our
Supreme Court found no conflict between our earlier denial of an appellate stay and the
election-timing concerns identified in Purcell v. Gonzalez, 549 U.S. 1 (2006), when "the
intent and effect of the order is to minimize disruption, preserve the status quo, and avoid
irreparable harm while the appellate court decides the substantive question of the
propriety of the lower court's injunction." Kansas Appleseed, No. 130,839, Order at 5.

       This is an important case, and important rights and interests are at stake on both
sides. Kansans have a substantial interest in the orderly administration of elections under
the law. Kansans also have a substantial interest in ensuring that well-meaning and
qualified Kansas voters are not deprived of their votes through an interim judicial ruling
that may later prove unnecessary.


       The importance of these competing interests counsels careful appellate review, not
a premature resolution of the merits through an interim stay motion. The parties and the
public are entitled to the benefit of the deliberative appellate process: briefing, considered


                                              7
argument, and a reasoned decision applying Kansas law to the issues presented. As the
Kansas Supreme Court observed when it declined emergency review earlier in this case,
adherence to the ordinary appellate process allows for "deeper exploration of, and
refinement of complex issues" and the orderly development of the law. Kansas
Appleseed, No. 130,839, Order at 8. We owe that process to both sides. Our decision
today does not forecast the ultimate disposition of the appeal.


       For now, our task is narrower. Considering the Secretary's preliminary showing on
the merits, the State's asserted injuries, the irreparable consequences a stay could impose
on voters, the public interest, and the rapidly approaching election calendar, we conclude
