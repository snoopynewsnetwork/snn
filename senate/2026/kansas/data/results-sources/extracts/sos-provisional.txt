     PROVISIONAL VOTING GUIDE
Provisional voting was introduced in Kansas in 1996 as a result of the National Voter Registration Act
(NVRA). Provisional voting provides individuals with the opportunity to vote even though questions
exist on their eligibility. Kansas law encourages individuals to participate in an election by allowing any
individual with an eligibility issue to cast a provisional ballot.

There are a number of instances where an individual may be provided with a provisional ballot. The
most common include:
 • Failure to provide photographic identification
 • Address change
 • Name change
 • Already issued a ballot

The Kansas Constitution (Article 5) and state law (K.S.A. 25-3701, 3702), allows registered voters who
have moved from one precinct to another, in Kansas, 30 days before an election to vote at the former
precinct in which they are registered. Unless there is a question about an individual’s eligibility, voters
can proceed by casting a regular ballot.

Individuals may contact their county election officer to determine if their provisional ballot was counted
and, if not, the reasoning.

HOW TO CAST A PROVISIONAL BALLOT
1. Before receiving a ballot, individuals must complete a new voter registration application form. This
   form will be attached to the provisional ballot envelope.
2. Individuals will then be allowed to complete their ballot. Provisional ballots are in the form of paper
   ballots, regardless of the type of voting equipment used in the county.
3. Once complete, the individual will place their ballot in the envelope, seal it and return the ballot to an
   election board worker.
4. The provisional ballot envelope will have the specific eligibility issue written on it. Two election board
   workers will sign the envelope and write the word “provisional” next to the individual’s name in the
   poll book.
5. Envelopes containing provisional ballots are secured and placed with other provisional ballots.
6. Provisional ballots are not counted on election day. They are set aside for consideration by the
   board of county canvassers who certifies the election results for each respective county.
