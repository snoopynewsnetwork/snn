# Alaska official Senate results: authority, public files and operational limits

Observed **2026-10-18 UTC / 2026-10-17 America/Chicago**. **The official homepage currently links a working results application, but its actual dataset is the certified August 18, 2026 primary. No linked November 2026 results dataset, Senate contest ID, zero report or live returns were established.** The current general-election ballot and counting rules are published and were inspected separately. This is source research and offline validation; no live collector or calibrated forecast has been implemented.

The [Division homepage](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/), [results landing](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/election-results/) and [linked application](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/enr26/) are the statewide authority chain. [Unit coverage](data/results-sources/unit-coverage.csv) preserves all **523 2024**, **522 2022**, and **523 August 2026 primary** native units as **1,568 distinct election-qualified records**. None is a borough/census-area allocation or a cross-year geography join.

## State, regional and municipal authority

The Division's [counting-system explanation](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/ballot-counting-process/counting-system-and-schedule/) says precinct scanners transmit to the Director's Office; hand-count precincts call their regional office, which submits the tallies to the Director. Early, absentee in-person, by-mail, electronic and questioned ballots are centrally counted by House District. Later RCV rounds occur at the Director's Office. The page currently describes 271 scanner and 131 hand-count precincts; these are equipment categories, not additional result feeds.

No independent clerk/vendor Senate channel was established. One Division publication chain serves the whole state and all 28 priority districts; inventing 28 county vendors would misrepresent Alaska's administration.

A material current operational change is the [September 16 Region IV notice](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/region-4-update-notice-of-mail-delivery-change-for-absentee-in-person-ballots-for-the-2026-reaa-and-general-election/): for the REAA and November general elections, Region IV absentee-in-person return mail is rerouted through Region II in Anchorage. The notice identifies about 75 primary ballots received after August certification and lists aggregate affected locations. This changes mail handling, not the ballot's native reporting geography. Do not count Anchorage processing as extra Region II votes or estimate November losses from the historical 75.

The app schema has top-level `contestNames`, `candidateNames`, `totals`, `districts`, `precincts`, `summaryReportUrl` and `precinctReportUrl`. Each contest uses `n` (index into contest names), `c` (candidate array), `c[].n` (index into candidate names), `c[].t` (candidate votes), `t` (candidate-vote total), `b` (ballots), `v` (registration), and, in aggregate views, `r/p` (reported/total precincts). These meanings are checked against both the script's display code and actual PDF/CSV totals. Arrays are election/snapshot-scoped indexes, not durable candidate or contest IDs. No method dimension, RCV round dimension, correction ledger or exact outstanding-candidate-vote field was established in these JSON files.

The detailed CSV preserves `Precinct_name`, `Pct_Id`, `Pct_seq_nbr`, `Aggregation_ID`, `Reporting_flag`, `Contest_Id`, `candidate_id`, `candidate_name`, `Party_Code`, repeated per-unit ballot/under/overvote totals, and five actual method-column groups. Do not sum repeated ballot fields once per candidate. The 2024 export has **80 blank `Pct_Id` values** for Early/Question units; retain those blanks and qualify sequence-based keys by election and contest. The 2022 text has labels/record ordinals, not equivalent native sequence IDs. The 2026 primary CSV has a trailing empty header field; the parser preserves the meaningful named columns and selects the exact Senate contest.

**Observed app coverage traps:** the August primary's 40 map district totals sum to **165,943**, exactly **61** below statewide Senate votes; HD99 supplies those 61. The 402 physical map precincts sum to only **114,349**, while the CSV's complete 523 units sum to **166,004**. These are alternative levels/slices. Adding map totals to CSV administrative rows without matching scope can duplicate votes. The 2024 district-map sum similarly needs the separate 401-vote HD99 record to reach the state. All 402 displayed physical-precinct candidate vectors were checked in both years, and all 40 August district vectors match the CSV.

**Completion is not remaining votes:** all 523 August CSV units have `Reporting_flag=0`, including 521 with positive ballots. Its physical Kipnuk and Kwigillingok rows have zero ballots; the official summary reports 401/403, but that alone does not establish a causal status mapping. Keep the flag as a raw field with unknown semantics. Neither zero flags, `certified`, nor `r/p` supplies an exact outstanding-vote estimate. The inspected 2024 Early Voting-labeled units for HD6, HD7, HD8 and HD37 contain actual Election Day columns, together **1,958 Election Day votes/1,965 ballots**; classification must use the columns, not the label. The [turnout method reconciliation](data/turnout/method-reconciliation.csv) preserves both views.

**New August CSV balance gap:** candidate sums match the PDF and JSON for every candidate, but **47 unit rows leave 88 ballots unexplained** after adding candidate votes and the CSV's explicit under/overvotes. State totals are 166,004 candidate votes + 753 reported undervotes + 147 reported overvotes = 166,904, versus 166,992 ballots. The exact [47-unit gap audit](data/results-sources/primary2026-ballot-balance-gaps.json) preserves labels, native sequences and original values. Every row's total still matches its five method columns. Cause is unverified; no extra candidate votes or replacement undervotes are invented. This limits invalid-vote analysis of the primary snapshot, not the independently reconciled 2024/2022 baselines.

The current roster additionally lists **certified Senate write-ins Sidney “Sid” Hill (Undeclared) and Heather McElwain (Registered Libertarian)**. These are distinct from the four printed candidates; the October 29 write-in filing deadline has not yet passed, so recheck the list before ingestion. McElwain's August raw party code is REP, whereas the current general-election roster says Registered Libertarian. Candidate registration affiliation does not establish party nomination or endorsement; the ballot explicitly makes that distinction. Rotation changes row order across HD ballots and must not change identity.

The Division's [current general-election/RCV explanation](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/election-information/#RankedChoice), the actual 2026 ballot, and [AS 15.15.350(c)–(g) in the Legislature's Title 15 PDF](https://www.akleg.gov/statutesPDF/Title-15.pdf) establish ranked choice for this election. The law elects a candidate with more than half of active ballots on the initial tabulation; otherwise it eliminates the lowest candidate and transfers to next continuing preferences, continuing to the final two. Inactive ballots, overvotes, sequential skips and ties require their stated rules. An initial plurality, a sum of Republican candidates, or a fixed D/R denominator does not determine the RCV winner. The legal active-ballot rule is more precise than the Division FAQ's shorthand majority wording.

The [24ESEG initiative status](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/petitions-and-ballot-measures/petition-status/?initiative_id=24ESEG#is81213) says it will appear on the general ballot; the [official hearing notice](https://aws.state.ak.us/OnlinePublicNotices/Notices/View.aspx?id=224503) identifies it as **2026 Ballot Measure No. 2**. The [updated August 28 official summary](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/AGO-No.-2025200007-24ESEG-updated-language-8.28.pdf) describes proposed repeal of open primaries, RCV and campaign-finance provisions. It is pending, not already effective. [Alaska Constitution XI §6](https://ltgov.alaska.gov/information/alaskas-constitution/) makes an initiated law effective 90 days after certification. Thus a November vote for repeal does not retrospectively convert this published ranked-choice Senate election to plurality.

Historical stage controls remain essential. The [2022 official first-choice summary](https://elections.alaska.gov/results/22GENR/ElectionSummaryReportRPT.pdf) contains **261,705 candidate/write-in votes on 267,047 ballots**. The [official RCV report](https://elections.alaska.gov/results/22GENR/US%20SEN.pdf) has a **266,797-ballot population**, with final Murkowski **136,330** and Tshibaka **117,534**, both REP. Its initial RCV allocation is not the literal scanner first-choice export. The companion [CVR coverage audit](data/baselines/cvr-ballot-coverage-gaps.csv) localizes the **250-ballot** difference: five absent physical precincts and six missing Levelock ballots, accounting for 237 first-choice candidate/write-in votes and 13 undervotes. Cause and missing rankings remain unknown. Do not splice full-population first choices into narrower-population final rounds. None of those candidates, party codes or historical IDs can be substituted for the actual November roster.

## Replacement, correction, access and remaining-vote handling

1. **Validate identity before any ingestion.** Follow the current official homepage/results landing anew. Require the actual November date, Senate title, full candidate names, stage, source status and complete native unit universe. A familiar `enr26` pathname, HTTP200, `format=2024`, or correct Senate title alone is insufficient. Preserve a zero/test label if one later appears. Reject a historical primary, HTML challenge or empty app shell as November votes.
2. **Replace cumulative snapshots.** App code assigns the newly fetched JSON to `electionResults`; it does not append a vote batch. Choose one authoritative observation per election/contest/stage/unit/method/time. State totals, HD totals, physical precincts, administrative method rows, summary PDFs, CSVs, maps and regional processing are overlapping views. Never sum them all, average them, or add successive fetches. When precinct data is loaded, the app replaces the statewide-only object with the full object. Preserve decreases and source corrections as revisions; no public correction event log or negative-change replay was established.
3. **Keep completion and freshness separate.** App `updated` and PDF print times have no explicit timezone. Store their literal text, UTC retrieval time, HTTP Date/Last-Modified/ETag/cache headers when present, source status and file hash. HTTP timestamps are transport metadata, not a substitute vote-count time. The inspected script implements a user-initiated Refresh Results button, cache-busting query strings, and hides refresh when certified. It contains no timed `setInterval`/`setTimeout` loop. A repeat August JSON fetch was byte-identical; [the two observations](data/results-sources/repeat-observation.json) are not a demonstrated election-night cadence. Polling interval/backoff remains an implementation choice, not a publisher guarantee.
4. **Retain all missing/administrative units.** Keep HD99 separate, 80 blank 2024 native IDs blank, original IDs/labels/sequences, source election and vintage. Do not polygon-join administrative method units. Do not infer method from labels where actual columns disagree. No 2022→2024, August→November or current 2026 boundary crosswalk was validated.
5. **Outstanding votes stay uncertain.** The [Division statistics page](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/research/statistics/) contains aggregate ballots issued/received by method and historical combined reports. Issued minus received includes ballots never returned; received includes accepted, pending, rejected and already counted categories unless explicitly distinguished. It is not exact remaining Senate candidate votes. Current August/2024 linked combined reports returned HTTP405 access pages in this audit, retained as failures. No November accepted-unprocessed Senate ballot ledger was established; remaining-candidate-vote fields stay null. Registration minus ballots, unreported-precinct percentage and past turnout do not supply that number.
6. **Manual fallback retains authority.** Open the Division's actual election page, follow its current PDF/CSV links, preserve the original file or dated screenshot and record election/stage/status/UTC, full candidate labels, unit/method coverage and any missing fields. Use the relevant regional office to resolve a counting category or geographic gap; a regional explanation is not additional votes. If current files disagree, retain both snapshots and quarantine unmatched values until scope/status is understood.

Python `requests` failed Alaska certificate validation in this environment; the [failure record](data/results-sources/python-requests-access.json) is retained. System `/usr/bin/curl` succeeded using **default TLS verification throughout**, without an insecure override. Raw current eCFR returned HTTP200 at an effective access-challenge host; DOT time pages returned raw HTTP403 although web rendering worked. These are recorded access limits, not valid legal/result content. Successful alternative primary routes were Legislature PDFs/current bill histories, the Division's actual 2026 pamphlet and ballot, and BTS's public time-zone service.
