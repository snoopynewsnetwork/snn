# Alaska collection, RCV and remaining-vote strategy

Observed **2026-10-18 UTC / 2026-10-17 Central**. This is a proposed operation, not an implemented collector or calibrated probability model. Read the [official inventory](official-results.md), [turnout geography](turnout.md), [baseline caveats](county-partisanship.md) and [regional news survey](local-news-results.md).

## Reporting geography and rounds

Alaska requires its native election units. Collect all official house-district, precinct and regional/administrative units identified in the inventory, including statewide or regional absentee/early/questioned aggregates where published. Use the measured 80% set for deep inspection and maps, while retaining every reporting unit in statewide arithmetic. Boroughs and census areas are not interchangeable with house districts. Never fabricate county totals from unmatched districts. Use county-style filenames only as documented compatibility names.

The reconciled historical coverage is **41 native groups per election**, HD1–40 plus separate HD99 federal-overseas administration, comprising **523 underlying 2024 units** and **522 in 2022**. The exact priority set is 28 HDs, totaling 271,492 / 338,177 presidential votes (80.28103626%); the [dispatch JSON](data/turnout/priority-set.json) retains ordered keys and constituents. Preserve HD99 in statewide sums without assigning it a physical polygon. Election-specific district numbers do not establish cross-year geographic equivalence.

The [Division's current election information](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/election-information/) confirms ranked choice general elections and publication of rounds/transfers alongside summary reports. Identify each candidate by official ID and ballot label, not only surname or party. Keep first-choice counts, elimination rounds, continuing-ballot denominator, exhausted/inactive ballots and final counts distinct. Two Republican finalists do not form a D/R baseline; an independent challenger is not automatically Democratic. The [actual November sample ballot](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/election/2026/General/SampleBallots/HD1-JD1.pdf) prints Gerald L. Heikes, Mary Peltola, Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. and Dan S. Sullivan. Preserve the two Sullivan identities separately. Daniel J. Jr. has no printed affiliation although the [current roster](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/candidates/?election=26genr) says Registered Republican; retain that discrepancy. Certified write-ins and their registration labels remain a separate list subject to the October 29 filing deadline. McElwain's August REP code differs from the current general-election Libertarian registration label. Recheck official identities before collection. The pending RCV repeal initiative is not effective law for this election; current ballot instructions and AS 15.15.350 govern. See the [verified rules and ballot inventory](official-results.md#november-ballot-identity-ranked-choice-and-possible-repeal).

A new RCV round is a new allocation of the same ballots. Never add round totals together or append later-round transfers as new voter turnout. Before RCV, scenario analysis must explicitly model elimination order, transfers and exhaustion when they can affect the result. A two-candidate tie calculator may be shown only as a clearly conditional scenario, not an Alaska election winner probability.

The validated 2022 CVR replay exactly reproduces every published round and transfer but covers **266,797 ballots**, compared with 267,047 in the complete first-choice publication. The 250-ballot gap is localized to five absent precincts and six missing Levelock ballots; their later ranks and the cause remain unknown. Do not impute them into final transfers. The final 136,330 Murkowski and 117,534 Tshibaka votes are both Republican. The separate historical Gross–Sullivan scenario preserves Gross's independent/nonpartisan identity alongside the raw `DEM` results code. Neither is a candidate-specific 2026 model.

The [downloaded map index](maps/index.html) provides 28 official PDF packets covering all 256 priority physical precincts and both baseline/current GIS snapshots, each with 402 statewide features. All 523/522 historical units remain in the compatibility inventory; administrative units receive no polygon allocation. Current retrieval does not establish November approval. Two invalid features in each snapshot and observed overlaps remain documented; neither geometry repairs nor cross-year equivalence are assumed.

## Source timing and proposed cadence

The [official 2026 calendar](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/calendar/), retained with retrieval/hash metadata in [news evidence](data/news/manifest.json), says November 3 polls run 7 a.m.–8 p.m. and web results follow after 9 p.m. It also describes an Election Day zero report. Alaska Public Media's [October explainer](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/elections/2026-10-05/what-to-know-about-alaskas-general-election) attributes an 8:45–9 p.m. first-release plan to officials. These differ slightly: use current official election-day notices for operations and treat either time as an expectation, not a guaranteed first result. Ordinary November poll close is 05:00 UTC November 4 in Alaska Standard Time and 06:00 UTC in the western Aleutian Hawaii-Aleutian zone. HD37 spans the issue; representative official BTS point checks do not establish a precinct timezone crosswalk. Electronic/fax deadlines explicitly stated in Alaska Time remain statewide deadlines. Check actual polling-place notices and later orders rather than assigning a single close to every HD. The [official calendar/pamphlet review](official-results.md#counting-calendar-deadlines-and-timezones) records the controlling sources and access limitations.

| Source | Proposed starting check interval | Conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified Division results index/version/timestamp | 5 minutes around an announced release, 15 minutes otherwise on election night | Resolve actual November links; zero/test reports stay excluded. Fetch detail when the official release changes. |
| Announced later absentee/questioned batches | 5–10 minutes during the release window, otherwise hourly while counting | Follow official notices; a lack of new votes is not an outage. |
| Official RCV publication | 5 minutes around its announced tabulation, then on change | Verify election, round, transfer accounting and exhaustion before replacing the displayed round. |
| Newsroom / NYT backstops | 5–10 minutes if verified/permitted; otherwise manual | Same geography, candidate and round; preserve supplier and time. |
| Quiet canvass period | Daily, plus announced reviews/publications | Continue until the official status resolves, with recount/correction notices tracked. |

The officially linked `enr26` app still contains the **August 18 primary**, not November results. Its linked JS/JSON/CSV are inspected historical/setup evidence only: neither pathname nor HTTP 200 validates the election. The client has a manual Refresh Results action, no observed timer, and a repeated JSON capture was byte-identical. Source publisher timestamps lack an explicit timezone. November feed paths, IDs and genuine update intervals remain unverified.

These are collection proposals. No observed timestamped sequence establishes a guaranteed publication cadence. Use host limits, jitter, conditional requests, explicit timeout/backoff and bounded concurrency. Poll one shared state release rather than independently downloading it once per precinct. For PDFs, retain changed bytes and document metadata; a new HTTP date does not prove new candidate totals.

## Provenance, correction and overlap

Use the [officially linked channels](official-results.md) as the count authority. Archive each changed response with source URL, UTC retrieval, HTTP headers, hash, publisher time/timezone, election/contest, status, native unit/method IDs and parser version. Preserve original bytes before parsing. Reject app shells, challenge HTML, empty/test playlists and old primary elections even under HTTP 200. Python Requests had a local issuer-chain failure on the Division site during research; system curl with default certificate verification succeeded. Preserve that transport distinction rather than calling the site unavailable or disabling validation.

Replace cumulative snapshots. Derive compatible changes afterward, retaining decreases, corrections and candidate reassignment. Validate candidate sums, reporting-unit coverage and round/transfer identities against the official statewide view. Never combine release vintages or add statewide, regional and precinct views that overlap. Early/absentee/questioned administrative units may not map to polygons: leave them explicitly unallocated until crosswalk evidence permits finer geography.

Use actual method columns rather than inferring voting method from a unit name. Four 2024 units labeled Early Voting contain Election Day counts as well. Some native precinct IDs are blank or reused; preserve the source sequence, contest, administrative label and election-specific key. Reporting-unit counts exclude or include administrative rows differently across products, so completion denominators require explicit scope. In the August snapshot, 40 map district totals omit 61 HD99 votes and 402 physical map precincts omit separate method votes: use the complete 523-unit CSV for full coverage. All CSV reporting flags are zero despite positive certified counts; their semantics are unestablished. The same primary CSV leaves **88 ballots unexplained across 47 units** after candidate, under- and overvote sums. Preserve this exact source gap; do not manufacture candidate votes or invalid-ballot replacements. It does not alter the reconciled 2024/2022 baseline totals.

A missing Division release can use a timestamped manual capture from that same official site. A newsroom count is a separately attributed fallback with an explicit substitution reason and reconciliation rule. The [news survey](local-news-results.md) records historical primary/test data at Alaska's News Source, a 2024 DDHQ product at the Beacon, and access gaps. Shared AKPM/KTOO reporting or common Division upstream data is not independent confirmation. NYT counts, calls, estimated completion and any forecast remain separate fields.

## What remains and when it can count

The [official calendar](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/calendar/) lists November 13 as the deadline for timely postmarked ballots mailed from within the United States, November 18 for those from overseas, and November 18 for the final regional absentee/questioned review. It lists November 25 as a certification **target**, not a statutory guarantee for that specific date. Verify changes and eligibility rules against current official notices. Late-arriving eligible ballots and questioned ballots are reasons to retain remaining-vote uncertainty after all polling places report.

The [official 2026 voter pamphlet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/election/2026/General/OEPbooks/2026%20Alaska%20General%20VP_Region%201_ENG_web.pdf), page 5, schedules November 3 first-choice reporting, November 4–17 ongoing counting and November 18 final tabulation/unofficial reporting. Exact RCV start hour and release frequency remain unverified. The official schedule places final regional absentee/questioned counts on November 18; the general RCV explanation puts subsequent rounds after the day-15 count. No general signature/witness cure window was established: proposed bills and rejection notification must not be treated as enacted cure rights. The [rules review](official-results.md#counting-calendar-deadlines-and-timezones) retains legislative status and current primary instructions. Separate receipt, eligibility review, scanning, first-choice release, RCV tabulation and certification. A reported precinct percentage is not the fraction of final votes counted.

The Division and its regional offices remain the count authority; municipal clerks are not automatically separate Senate feeds. A September Region IV mail-routing notice describes processing through Anchorage without changing the ballot's native geography. Preserve processing location separately. The [statistics page](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/research/statistics/) has issued/received counts, but inspected combined-report downloads returned HTTP 405 and no November accepted-unprocessed Senate ledger was established. Issued minus received, registration minus turnout, and reporting-precinct percentages are not exact remaining candidate votes.

For each actual reporting unit/method, record known counted votes and the source/time of outstanding estimates. Use low/base/high ranges; missing remaining-vote evidence stays unknown. Compare each partial return with the same native geographic/method slice and boundary vintage. When ballots are only published regionally, retain regional uncertainty rather than attributing them to physical precincts by population. Keep counted votes fixed and simulate only remaining ballots and, where relevant, their preference transfers/exhaustion.

## Rehearsal and readiness gates

Build a coverage board with every official unit, last fetch, last change, first-choice/round status, matched geometry, known votes and outstanding uncertainty. Prove replay of genuine saved releases, idempotent retries, correction decreases, overlapping aggregates, new administrative IDs, RCV-round replacement and exhausted-ballot accounting. Manual entries need source bytes/screenshot, operator/observation time and the exact unit/round.

The first deliverable is reliable count collection and transparent scenarios. Publishing a win probability requires a dated prior, candidate-specific transfer/turnout assumptions, genuine historical timestamped replays and held-out calibration under the [root plan](../../../ELECTION_NIGHT_PLAN.md). Synthetic ordering can stress-test the code but cannot calibrate Alaska's delayed batches or RCV outcomes. Keep probability unavailable until those checks pass.
