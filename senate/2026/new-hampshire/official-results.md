**Authority and publication scope.** The moderator conducts the public local count and announces the result; the clerk certifies and forwards the local return; SOS examines and totals all constituent returns. Under the current statute, paper and electronic returns follow completion of the count, ordinarily reach SOS by 8 a.m. the following day unless SOS orders another time, and the local copy is open to inspection. The current law requires the clerk to post return copies within seven days. The separate moderator certificate/checklist/ballots forms generally have 48-hour requirements. None of these deadlines is a verified public web refresh interval. Federal certificates are issued by the governor under the recount/appeal conditions, rather than by an online percentage counter. **Winner, write-ins and ties.** RSA 659:81 uses the highest vote count for the single office, not a majority threshold. A candidate must finish strictly above every other candidate; a two-candidate tie is not a win, and Laplante and valid write-in candidates cannot be dropped from the legal comparison. RSA 659:64/65 govern intent and defective ballots. Under 659:64-a/67, a write-in for a printed candidate is incorporated into that candidate's total, with one vote for the same person on one ballot. Under 659:73 the return separately reports other named write-in recipients and their sum, excluding votes already assigned to printed candidates. A generic historical mark count cannot substitute for that adjudicated named universe. A tie for largest vote for U.S. Senate triggers another election called by the governor with advice of the council under 660:25–26, repeated if necessary. [RSA 659](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/659/659-mrg.htm), [RSA 660:25–26](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/660/660-mrg.htm).

**Ordinary in-person and absentee scope.** Qualified voters must meet age, citizenship and local domicile requirements; election-day registration is available subject to qualification verification. The current statutory text and the court orders below must be read together. New Hampshire does not provide general no-excuse early voting. RSA 657:1 provides excuse-based absentee eligibility, including specified absence, religious, disability and employment/care reasons and qualified winter-storm circumstances. Current 657:17-c requires an absentee applicant's qualifying photo-ID copy, notarized signature, or in-person qualifying-ID verification before issuance. A qualifying delivery agent is governed by 657:17, not an arbitrary bearer. Ordinary completed absentee ballots must reach the clerk by **5 p.m. election day**, rather than merely be postmarked; 657:22 specifically excepts emergency workers under 657:21-a,V and disabled voters appearing under 659:20-a. Those exceptions are not a general late-arrival reservoir. [RSA 654](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/654/654-mrg.htm), [RSA 657](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/657/657-mrg.htm), [RSA 659:20-a](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/659/659-mrg.htm), [current Lebanon absentee guidance](https://www.lebanonnh.gov/564/Absentee-Ballots-and-Voting).

RSA 659:49 ordinarily starts absentee processing at 1 p.m. or a properly noticed earlier time at least two hours after opening; processing is not authority to publish a final count before polls close. Timely later receipts must still be processed. The separately signed absentee certificate and ballot/checklist/reconciliation forms measure administrative scope; the individual absentee-status lookup is not a public candidate-results or aggregate remaining-vote feed. No current November public UOCAVA late-arrival tail, general post-election cure pool, provisional-ballot count or exact outstanding quantity was verified. This is an explicit operational gap, not a claim all such categories equal zero. [RSA 659:49–53](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/659/659-mrg.htm), [RSA 657:22–26](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/657/657-mrg.htm), [SOS uniformed/overseas channel; direct access failed](https://www.sos.nh.gov/elections/frequently-asked-questions/uniformed-and-overseas-voters).

**Recount, canvass and certification.** RSA 660:1 permits a candidate's written recount application by the Friday after election (November 6 for this election) when the specified difference is below 20% of total ballots cast in the constituent towns; fees vary under 660:2. This is a request-based rule, not an automatic recount threshold. The process must begin by the Wednesday following the request deadline under 660:4; visual ballot inspection, protests, declaration and appeal provisions follow in 660:3–6. SOS canvass waits for all constituent returns under 659:81; the governor's Senate certificate follows 659:84's recount/appeal expiration or written-waiver conditions. An election-night announcement, full website geography coverage and a certified final result are different statuses. [RSA 660:1–6](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/660/660-mrg.htm), [RSA 659:81/84](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/659/659-mrg.htm).
