## Current legal authority, clocks and outstanding-vote meaning

The complete primary [Election Act chapter](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/laws-index/chap32-full.html), enacted [LB1075](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/FloorDocs/109/PDF/Slip/LB1075.pdf), [SOS calendar](https://sos.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/doc/elections/2026/2026_Election_Calendar.pdf), [voter ID guidance](https://sos.nebraska.gov/voter-id), and [military guidance](https://sos.nebraska.gov/elections/military-voters) were acquired. [Legal findings](data/results-sources/legal-findings.json) retain section-specific evidence and limitations. The web chapter contains prospective January 2027 provisions: publication today is not synonymous with operative November law.

General Senate outcome is plurality/highest vote total, with state-board lot resolution for equal highest votes under §§32-1038(2), 32-1122(5). Do not apply primary nomination rules or an assumed runoff. Under current §32-1119, for more than 500 votes cast the relevant one-winner recount margin is at most **1% of the leading candidate's votes**, subject to waiver, not 1% of all ballots. State-filed recounts begin 9 AM on the fifth Wednesday (December 2). Current §32-1121, amended by LB1075 §39 operative April 16, requires a losing-candidate recount request by the **third day after canvass concludes**; the older calendar says five days. Preserve the conflict and use the later operative statute. A requested state-filed recount can use the sixth Wednesday if the fifth cannot be used.

County canvass notice is due before noon the next day, with at least 24 hours between notice and convening after early count duties; obvious errors may be corrected with a signed explanation (§32-1031). County abstracts are due the third Monday, November 23; electronic/fax originals by the fourth Wednesday, November 25 (§§32-1034–1035). State canvass meets the fourth Monday, November 30, and may adjourn until returns complete (§32-1037). Unofficial webpage votes are distinct from the signed/sealed state abstract (§32-1038).

The chapter's contest grounds/procedures (§§32-1101–1117) are retained, but §32-1102's listed state-office venue and §32-1109's political-subdivision 20-day notice should **not automatically be labeled U.S. Senate contest deadlines**. [Article I §5](https://www.senate.gov/civics/constitution_item/constitution.htm) makes the Senate judge its members' elections/returns/qualifications. Comprehensive later litigation and any Senate-specific contest proceeding remain an external legal qualification.

Under §32-908, Central areas vote **8 AM–8 PM local**, Mountain areas **7 AM–7 PM local**, with those in line at closing allowed to finish. Nebraska's boundary follows [49 CFR §71.7(c), (g)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-A/part-71/section-71.7), including a section/range route through **Cherry**, which spans both zones; do not give all Cherry reporting units a single timezone. Eastern Douglas/Lancaster/Sarpy/Adams/Madison are Central; Dawes/Box Butte/Scotts Bluff/Cheyenne/Kimball/Keith/Chase/Perkins are Mountain. After November 1's DST transition, both ordinary closes correspond to **02:00 UTC November 4**, absent an operative extension. Source display CT, vendor CDT and zone-unspecified local PDFs remain separate literal clocks. No current per-native time-zone/polling-place join is approved.

Ordinary early ballots must **arrive by local close of polls**, rather than merely be postmarked (§§32-908, 32-950). A signed oath/sealed return goes to the issuing county under §32-949; do not assume polling-place drop-off is an authorized return. Ordinary mailed ballot requests are due close of business October 23 (§32-947); in-office early voting is October 5 through November 2 (§32-942/calendar). Agent pickup must occur by one hour before polls close and return by closing (§32-943). Applications, issued ballots, received envelopes, accepted envelopes and tabulated contest votes are different populations.

Photo ID or the statutory alternative applies to in-person voting; mail application can use Nebraska license/state-ID number, acceptable ID copy or reasonable-impediment certification (§32-941). Missing/unverified application ID holds issuance pending provision. Return oath signature and registration/address checks are required (§32-1027); reasonable-impediment signature matching is explicitly required. Do not turn this into a fabricated universal post-election envelope-signature cure. **November ID-provisional cure is in person by Tuesday, November 10**, consistent with current SOS guidance and pre-2027 §§32-915.03/32-1002.01. LB1075 §§18/29 change Tuesday to Friday only **January 1, 2027**, under §67; complete pages 11/24 were visually inspected. Ordinary provisional registration/residence/correct-precinct/no-duplicate/signed-document checks under §32-1002 are distinct; investigation is within seven business days. No general ordinary-envelope post-election cure extension was established.

Early envelope preparation may begin the second Friday before election; discretionary counting no earlier than 24 hours before polls open (§32-1027), with no release before closing (§32-1013). These permissions do not prove any particular county's timing. Madison does publish a specific November release plan: 8 PM early, 10:30 PM first Election Day set, midnight second set, periodic thereafter excluding provisional; its [original schedule](https://madisoncountyne.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/General-Election-Result-Release-Schedule.pdf) was downloaded and viewed. It prints no timezone. That plan is not observed cadence.

Emergency responders outside county have a distinct noon election-day request and authorized-transmission route (§32-939.03).
