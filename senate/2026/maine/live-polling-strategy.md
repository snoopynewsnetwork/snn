# Maine municipal collection and ranked-choice strategy

Research observed **2026-10-18 UTC / 2026-10-17 Central**. This is a proposed operating strategy, not a deployed collector or calibrated win probability. Use the [official inventory](official-results.md), [turnout geography](turnout.md), [baseline scenarios](county-partisanship.md) and [news survey](local-news-results.md).

## Municipal first choices and central tabulation

Collect every native reporting municipality/unit in the reconciled baseline. Maine's towns, cities, plantations and other reporting units must not be replaced with a 16-county-only calculation. County rollups may be derivatives after exact unit matching. The measured 80% set prioritizes deep source/map work; all units remain in statewide arithmetic. Preserve native names/IDs, jurisdiction and precinct combinations, unorganized-territory treatment and unmatched units explicitly.

The minimal **164-unit** priority set totals 658,029 (80.142667%); first 163 total only 656,778, and Pownal's 1,251 crosses the threshold. The Secretary of State's [September 4 process update](https://www3.maine.gov/sos/news/general-election-two-months-away-secretary-states-office-provides-update-maine-election-1) says counting begins when polls close at 8 p.m., municipalities report at voting places on election night, and clerks have two days to submit results to the state. Early absentee **processing** is not early release of candidate counts. That timeline does not establish a single immediate statewide municipal API. Use verified clerk publications and explicit manual fallback where feeds are unavailable.

The exact 164 priorities have individual source audits; 155 have successful direct official route/document bytes and nine retain direct-access gaps. The remaining 348 are inventoried with discovery-level municipal tests. Successful page access does not establish November returns.

The [current RCV resources](https://www.maine.gov/sos/elections-voting/upcoming/ranked-choice-voting-tabs) include a November Senate sample ballot. Check the actual candidate configuration and any replacements, rather than retaining primary nominees by assumption.

The [October 15 declared write-in PDF](https://www3.maine.gov/sos/sites/maine.gov.sos/files/inline-files/Nov26%20Declared%20Write%20in%20List%20-%20Updated%2010.15.26.pdf) names Andrews, Olson, Oswald, Steinberger, Turcotte and Webb for Senate, with distinct exact labels retained in [candidate evidence](data/results-sources/candidates-2026.json). The landing anchor still displayed an older date; use the actual document vintage. The inspected sample is one Cumberland style with two printed candidates, one generic write-in row and three ranking columns. It does not prove every ballot style, the eventual live candidate-ID schema, or an exhaustive no-exclusions bulletin. Recheck current candidate/exclusion notices and source-specific write-in scope before real ingestion.

An independent historical Senate winner stays independent. A conditional named-opposition scenario must be shown separately from D/R calculations, with explicit coalition/transfer assumptions. Before RCV, first-choice D/R shares alone do not determine the winner. Model elimination order, transfers and exhaustion if those can affect the result; no simple uniform D-share shift establishes an RCV win probability.

The current [§723-A](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/21-A/title21-Asec723-A.html) first-choice majority test uses ballots for the office, including first-rank blanks, overvotes and excluded-candidate rankings. Track that denominator separately from valid candidate totals. Subsequent rounds distinguish continuing candidates/ballots and exhaustion; with two or fewer continuing candidates, the most votes wins. These 2025-amended rules require verifying the actual current candidate/exclusion state. A D/R-only majority of valid votes is insufficient to resolve a multicandidate contest or this first-choice legal test. Later-round totals replace their own round scope; they never add to municipal first choices.

## Proposed collection checks

| Channel | Proposed initial interval | Admission condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified high-volume municipal source | 2–5 minutes while active | Current November Senate election and native unit; source limit/access verified. |
| Other municipal PDF/landing/manual lane | 5–10 minutes while counting | Exact document, status, publisher/observation time and coverage. |
| State receipt/certification/RCV publications | 5–10 minutes around announced releases; otherwise hourly | Confirm actual publication and round; do not assume real-time statewide completeness. |
| BDN, other regional products and NYT | 5–10 minutes if verified/permitted, otherwise manual | Current November identity, compatible town/round, supplier attribution. |
| Quiet post-election review | Daily plus announced tabulations/recounts | Follow current state and clerk notices until status resolves. |

These are proposed request intervals, not demonstrated publisher cadence. BDN's inspected historical component polls every 30 seconds, but that is browser behavior. Use jitter, conditional requests, bounded per-host concurrency, timeouts and backoff. Many municipal sources share hosting: do not burst every town at once. Show last successful fetch separately from last changed votes. An unchanged source during a counting pause is not necessarily unhealthy.

## Provenance, source precedence and overlap

Archive changed raw responses before interpreting them: URL, retrieval UTC, HTTP metadata, hash, publisher timestamp/timezone, election/contest/candidate, municipality/reporting-unit/method, round, status and parser version. Reject challenge HTML, app shells, test results, empty data and primary elections even when HTTP succeeds. Search-index text is discovery evidence, not a current results snapshot.

Prefer the coherent official clerk observation for a municipality and current round, then the state view when it incorporates or supersedes that unit at a known status/version. An attributed newsroom fallback needs a reason/time and a replacement rule. The [news survey](local-news-results.md) records BDN's clerk-collection/DDHQ chain, AP references at WGME, primary/test displays at WABI and unverified NYT automation. Shared sources can explain agreement; do not count brand names as independent confirmations.

Snapshots are cumulative. Replace observations, then derive compatible batch differences. Retain decreases, candidate corrections, revised municipal totals and RCV-round changes. Reconcile municipal sums to the state candidate view at compatible versions; never add a state total to towns it already contains, or add different publishers' cumulative totals. Administrative or combined units remain unallocated to polygons until the crosswalk is defensible. Keep unknown coverage visible rather than filling it with zero.

The tested BDN primary sample demonstrates why IDs/completion need explicit validation. All 514 VCU rows reconcile to their 16 county arrays, but those are 813 votes below the topline. Twenty-seven VCU IDs are null; two positive Madawaska rows reuse ID 5142, with no ward split identified. Keep every original row and source-array locator, mark uncertain identity and reconcile before any grouping. A five-digit local code is not county FIPS. Placeholder zero method fields, 514/514 completion, a call timestamp for an absent candidate ID and `uncontested:true` beside four candidates cannot establish source completeness. These primary observations stay quarantined from November data.

The 2024 archived Legislature workbooks match the House Clerk vintage but differ from the current corrected SOS workbooks solely at Alfred among disjoint native rows. Candidate totals rise by 290 Senate / 386 president while blanks fall equally; ballot totals remain fixed. York/state footers carry the same differences. The [independent coordinator cached-cell comparison](data/coordinator-vintage-review.json) preserves exact source hashes, changed cells and document clocks. This localizes the difference; it does not prove its cause or publication chronology. The current corrected SOS is the selected baseline, with NARA's named presidential controls as an additional check. Do not average the two vintages, force current counts to the Clerk, or assume a ballot-total match proves candidate agreement.

Municipal publications provide a separate version check. Of 14 retained local Senate candidate comparisons, 12 match the corrected SOS. The [Gorham official PDF](https://www.gorhammaine.gov/sites/g/files/vyhlif4456/f/uploads/election_results_11-05-2024.pdf) has King 5,810 and Kouzounas 3,507, respectively 400 and 30 below current SOS; its blank total is four lower. The [Falmouth summary](https://www.falmouthme.org/DocumentCenter/View/622) has King and Kouzounas one lower each and omits blanks/ballots. These differences are independent of the archived-SOS Alfred comparison; neither correction causes nor release order are established. Preserve the selected statewide baseline and each local observation's original scope. In [Rockport's summary](https://rockportmaine.gov/vertical/sites/%7B6F0724F7-400D-4D0B-B299-FF5E21F5B92A%7D/uploads/November_2024_-Results.pdf), the generic Senate Write-in row total 49 combines Write-In column 3 with Over/Under 46. It cannot supply 49 named write-in votes. The [local controls](data/results-sources/local-controls.json) and original previews retain these fields without allocation.

## What remains and readiness

The official [August absentee notice](https://www3.maine.gov/sos/news/maine-voters-can-request-absentee-ballots-nov-3-election-starting-today-ballots-will-be-mailed) says November absentee ballots must reach the voter's municipality by 8 p.m. November 3. Recheck any updated rules/notices before use. Received, accepted, processed, counted and published ballots are different states. A closed polling place or reported precinct need not imply all eligible ballots in its municipality have been included.

The [current statute/source review](official-results.md#current-rules-and-public-outstanding-ballot-evidence) establishes ordinary 8 p.m. Eastern standard closing on November 3 as November 4 01:00 UTC, subject to actual local exceptions/extensions. Municipal first-choice filing is within two business days, with the explicit second-day courier rule; state tabulation/certified submission is within 20 days except pending recounts. Do not promise a central RCV start or completion date: no November schedule was established. Ordinary absentee cures depend on the defect/method and use the return deadline; no general post-election cure window or UOCAVA postmark grace is assumed.

The state voter-data product concerns requests/issues/returns, warns of municipal entry lag and includes UOCAVA. The retained October 16 response is only a 65,536-byte HTTP range of a 16,143,837-byte file; its 23-column actual header differs from the published layout. It cannot establish a full statewide status census. Even a complete administrative file would need duplicate/replacement, eligibility, challenge, rejection and tabulation semantics before supplying an uncounted Senate ballot range. No exact outstanding count is inferred here.

For spatial comparisons, the [downloaded map package](precinct-maps.md) provides 155 municipal references, eight combined-component references and the administrative no-polygon entry. Most polygons are town/township boundaries; only the separately documented supplements supply finer ward/precinct references. Millinocket/Wilton historical township membership, the full Louds Island reporting extent, blocked Gorham/Ellsworth finer maps and nine additional finer-map gaps remain explicit. Eight invalid state polygons and three positive-area overlap pairs are retained without repairs. No 2024/2026 legal map approval or results/registration/board crosswalk is established; do not color these references as allocated precinct votes.

Keep counted candidate votes fixed. Estimate only remaining ballots and, where relevant, their transfers/exhaustion, with low/base/high ranges, evidence source and observation time. Compare incoming counts to the same municipality, method, geography vintage and round where possible. Missing comparable slices require coarser comparisons and wider uncertainty. Never infer voter preference from party enrollment or assign remaining administrative ballots geographically by population alone.

The coverage board must enumerate all official units, source status, last fetch/change, observed counts by round, matched geometry, unallocated units and remaining uncertainty. Manual captures retain source bytes/screenshot, exact values/units/round, operator and observation time. Rehearse cumulative replacement, duplicate retries, corrected decreases, renamed units, incomplete towns, feed outages, RCV-round transitions and exhaustion accounting with genuine saved releases.

A bounded official format test uses **June Democratic CD2**, not November Senate. The [retained audit](data/results-sources/rcv-audit.json) keeps all 425 initial-workbook native rows, including two rows with blank office/district fields. Round 1 has 81 more candidate votes and 81 fewer blank/exhausted than the initial publication, with the same 83,480 ballots; no transfer mechanism is inferred from that delta. A UOCAVA CVR subset has 345 rows but 342 distinct IDs, including three exact repeated pairs; it also differs from the initial UOCAVA Wood/blank counts by +2/−2 with equal ballots. Preserve all source rows, flag repeated IDs, and keep initial, central-round and CVR scopes distinct. Automatic deduplication, blank-to-zero coercion or forcing different stages to reconcile would change the evidence. This subset is a schema/scope test, not a statewide CVR replay or November calibration.

The first milestone is trustworthy collection and transparent conditional scenarios. For a genuinely two-candidate conditional scenario with counted lead `L` and positive expected remaining major-party votes `U`, `(1-L/U)/2` is the share needed to tie; it is not a probability or a general RCV solution. Publishing probabilities requires the genuine historical replay, held-out calibration and remaining-vote/transfer evidence in the [root plan](../../../ELECTION_NIGHT_PLAN.md). Synthetic ordering tests software but cannot calibrate Maine's reporting order or RCV outcome.
