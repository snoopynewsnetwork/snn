## Proposed request cadence

| Channel | Starting check interval | Required evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified statewide source | 3 minutes | Actual November Senate special-election identity, non-test status and coherent county candidate records; this proposed interval aligns with the advertised refresh, not measured count arrivals. |
| Priority county with a distinct verified publication | 2–5 minutes | Source limits and actual observed update rate; download only changed versions when possible. |
| Other counties / PDFs / manual captures | 5–10 minutes during active counting | Exact document time/status and geographic coverage; missing is not zero. |
| Newsroom / NYT comparison | 5–10 minutes if permitted and verified, otherwise manual | Compatible election, county, candidate and release time, with supplier attribution. |
| Post-election board activity | Hourly during announced releases, otherwise daily | Posted county/state canvass, cure, UOCAVA and recount notices. |

These are proposed collection intervals, **not demonstrated publisher cadence**. Apply per-host concurrency limits, jitter, conditional requests, explicit timeout and backoff. Display last successful fetch separately from last changed votes. Do not call a healthy unchanged source stale merely because counting has paused. Reconfirm wall-clock timezone and voting extensions using current official notices; preserve source timestamps literally until their timezone semantics are known.

The state advertises a 180-second XML refresh. Two saved mock XML releases differ only in `GeneratedDate`, with unchanged counts/sequence; a clock tick is not a vote update. Cuyahoga's inspected client checks metadata every 120 seconds, also not demonstrated publication cadence. [Directive 2026-48](https://www.ohiosos.gov/assets/dir2026-48-canvass-and-certification-of-the-november-3-2026-general-election.pdf) prescribes initial absentee submissions by 8 p.m. with allowances and half-hour submissions from 8:15 p.m. for all 32 priorities. That future state-submission schedule neither guarantees county-vendor timing nor requires waiting to submit final tallies. County initial absentee uploads must show zero precincts reporting even when they contain votes. Summary results, not precinct candidate detail, are required for the state's unofficial canvass.

## Precedence, raw evidence and correction handling

Choose one authoritative observation per unit/time. Prefer a coherent official state county record by default; use a county board publication when it demonstrably supersedes or explains the state view. A manually captured official count keeps official attribution. A newsroom substitution is a separate explicitly labeled fallback, with reason, source/time and a reconciliation rule. Never add state totals to their constituent county totals or add county summary counts to precinct detail covering the same ballots.

Archive original changed response bytes before parsing, with URL, retrieval UTC, publisher time/timezone where verified, HTTP metadata, body hash, election/contest/unit/method/candidate IDs, status and parser version. Reject challenge HTML and app shells under HTTP 200 as well as mock, primary, neighboring-state and wrong-office data. Inspect file internals: WCPO's downloaded XML sample has a September run date, older primary-named race fields and native jurisdiction IDs; those IDs must not be assumed to be county FIPS. Its surrounding results page warns of testing.

Cumulative snapshots replace earlier observations. Derive compatible batch differences afterward; retain decreases, reassigned candidates and precinct corrections as real revisions. Reconcile county candidate sums with the state view and method/precinct subtotals where supported. Inconsistent releases remain flagged for review rather than averaged or forced to agree. Keep unmatched or combined precincts and administrative absentee/early/provisional units visible; their counts need not have geographic polygons.

The historical tests demonstrate concrete revision/scope cases. Franklin's December 6, 2024 audit amendment reduces Brown and Moreno one vote each relative to the original state baseline; retain both vintages rather than rewrite the shared baseline. Clermont's 66 explicitly invalid write-ins explain its different local total. Wayne's 2024-labelled file contains 2022 results and is excluded. These are saved reproducible checks, not inferred rules about future corrections.

## Outstanding votes and comparisons

Counted votes stay fixed. Forecast only remaining votes with low/base/high ranges, evidence source and observation time. Track received, accepted, cured, scanned and published ballots distinctly. The website defines UOCAVA outstanding as requested but not returned; the directive uses issued but not yet counted. Carry each definition rather than merge those fields. Supplemental statistics can lag candidate uploads and the page says ballots counted populates after the county unofficial canvass. None supplies an exact accepted-unprocessed Senate inventory. A count of issued ballots is not a guaranteed future valid Senate vote, and precinct completion is not vote completion.

Current rules require ordinary absentee receipt by 7:30 p.m. November 3, without the former ordinary-mail grace period. Eligible UOCAVA receipt can extend through November 7, subject to the signed-by-close requirement; absentee/provisional cures and county eligibility decisions have separate rules. County canvass begins November 8–18 and certification is due by November 24; an exact state certification appointment was not established. The [official-results review](official-results.md) links current statutes/directives and retains explicit extension/superseding-law gates. Do not assume daily public count releases between unofficial completion and certification or convert every curable/issued ballot to a valid Senate vote.

Compare returns to the same county, boundary vintage and voting-method slice of the historical baseline. An early absentee release does not estimate Election Day preference by itself. Where precise outstanding units or crosswalks are unavailable, compare at a coarser supported geography and widen uncertainty. Retain independent turnout-composition and candidate-preference sensitivities; a 2024 presidential turnout mix is not the 2026 Senate electorate.

The [downloaded maps](precinct-maps.md) retain GIS geometry for 20 priority counties, 205 complete PDFs/466 pages, two images and a separately labeled Census 2020 reference. Original true curves and 84 empty/66 invalid geometry records remain unchanged; overlaps and unmatched IDs are diagnostics, not repaired joins. Richland's county packet could not be downloaded; Tuscarawas has an official raster but no vector geometry; Geauga D-23 is missing geometry. Census alternatives supply no current approval. Use county-level comparison until a supported current results-to-boundary crosswalk exists; never allocate omitted write-ins, split-piece votes or administrative units onto polygons by name/count coincidence.

The [reviewed conditional baselines](county-partisanship.md) retain all 88 counties and 196,288 other 2024 Senate votes. At fixed 2024 major-party weights the tie shift is +1.873834 D-share points, and D+1 two-party margin is +2.373834 share points. A two-party +1 margin is only 0.965591 all-candidate margin points at fixed other votes; a full +1 all-candidate margin needs different arithmetic. These are historical joint scenarios, not necessary county thresholds or a 2026 forecast. Observed 2022/2018-amended/2024 volumes supply sensitivity anchors with explicit composition; the middle volume is not a forecast median. The 2018 original and Miami-amended publications remain distinct, with the January 2019 correction retained as a real revision.

For counted statewide D-minus-R lead `L` and positive expected remaining major-party votes `U`, the conditional statewide tie threshold is `(1-L/U)/2`. For selected county `c`, use `(U_c-L-sum(other remaining net votes))/(2*U_c)` only when `U_c>0`. Below-zero and above-one thresholds mean already secured or impossible under the stated assumptions; they are not probabilities. Model other candidates separately where their victory or share can matter. Never turn the deterministic tie threshold into an uncalibrated win percentage.
