# Ohio special Senate election: official reporting sources and limits

Observed **October 17, 2026 America/Chicago / October 18 UTC**. Ohio’s November 3 election fills the **unexpired U.S. Senate term ending January 3, 2029**. **The working statewide reporting service currently serves an explicitly marked mock election. No genuine November returns were observed.** Several county November PDFs are zero reports, placeholders or unavailable; several working county applications still contain the May primary. This package documents tested public sources and offline reconciliations, not a functioning live collector or forecast.

The [89-authority inventory](data/official-results-sources.csv) covers the Secretary of State and **all 88 county boards of elections**. The exact **32-county priority set** was inspected through each actual county authority route and all 32 individually fetched state county JSON views, with local links followed where accessible. Successful schema tests, historical samples and exact local access gaps are distinguished below. The other 56 counties received authority/landing inspection; two malformed state URIs with trailing whitespace (Huron and Ottawa) were retried at the identical trimmed routes; their local vendors and November schemas are **not** claimed to have been deeply tested.

## Authority, election and candidate identity

Ohio’s [BOE directory](https://www.ohiosos.gov/directories/county-boards-of-elections) identifies an election board in each county and state oversight. The [official local-results directory](https://www.ohiosos.gov/directories/local-election-results) supplies county channels. Their raw requests returned access challenges here; research-browser rendering was available. Independently, the downloaded state XML publishes actual county authority URIs. Both discovery paths are retained. Some state URIs are county homepages, archives, or direct vendor pages, rather than current election feeds. They require following the actual local links and checking the returned election.

The state’s [data-portal explanation](https://data.ohiosos.gov/portal/election-dashboards) distinguishes election-night publication at [Ohio Decides](https://liveresults.ohiosos.gov/) from the certified historical dashboard. A local county-only view, the state’s county view and its statewide total describe overlapping votes. Municipalities and multicounty school/other districts appear in local reports but are not extra independent Senate election authorities. “Most populous county,” “overlap” and district-wide reports can contain other counties’ votes; retain their scope and never add them to county-only results.

The [special-election writ/directive](https://www.ohiosos.gov/assets/directive-2025-54-writ-of-special-election-for-united-states-senate-election.pdf), [Directive 2026-45](https://www.ohiosos.gov/assets/dir2026-45-form-of-the-official-ballot-for-the-november%203-general-election.pdf) and its [actual November sample ballot](https://www.ohiosos.gov/assets/dir2026-45-official-sample-ballot-november-3-2026.pdf) identify the unexpired term. ### Publication clocks and the published reporting schedule

The live page advertises an XML refresh every **three minutes**, and its script uses an optional 180-second refresh timer. That is a configured/advertised refresh behavior, not measured vote-arrival cadence. Two retained XML fetches, at 01:45:10 and 01:51:18 UTC, have `GeneratedDate`01:42:11 Z and 01:51:11 Z respectively. Their bodies differ only in that timestamp; counts and sequence 0 are unchanged. [Repeat comparison](data/results-sources/repeat-observation.json) preserves both. A changed generated clock cannot by itself signify a changed count. XML clocks explicitly ending `Z` are UTC; local PDF print times and JSON fields without offsets remain source-local strings. HTTP Date, Last-Modified, cache headers and UTC fetch time are separate transport observations.

[Directive 2026-48, printed pages 3–5](https://www.ohiosos.gov/assets/dir2026-48-canvass-and-certification-of-the-november-3-2026-general-election.pdf) establishes a **future county-submission schedule**: CSS login by 7:45 p.m., initial absentee upload by 8 p.m. with stated technical/partial-upload allowances, then assigned half-hour or hourly submissions. The [all-88 county schedule](data/results-sources/published-submission-schedule.json) and inventory preserve each assignment. **All 32 priority counties are in its 44-county half-hour cohort**, beginning 8:15 p.m., then 8:45,9:15 and so on. The remaining counties are split between hourly top-of-hour and bottom-of-hour groups; the latter also report initially at 8 p.m. Final tallies need not wait for an assigned slot. These requirements concern county submissions to the state, and do not guarantee that a county’s separate vendor updates on that schedule. They have not yet been observed operating on November 3.

## Working county vendor and historical reconciliation

### Enhanced Voting: Cuyahoga and Montgomery

Cuyahoga’s [official elections landing](https://boe.cuyahogacounty.gov/elections) and Montgomery’s [official results page](https://www.montgomery.boe.ohio.gov/election-results/) link Enhanced Voting. The downloaded application revealed its actual public API base `/results/public/api` and jurisdiction/election/data methods. Both actual catalogs were fetched: [Cuyahoga](https://app.enhancedvoting.com/results/public/api/jurisdictions/Cuyahoga-County-OH) and [Montgomery](https://app.enhancedvoting.com/results/public/api/jurisdictions/Montgomery-County-OH). Neither lists a November 2026 election. Their latest entries are May 5 primary elections; Montgomery’s election slug even ends `-Live`, which does not make it a November general election.

Complete public data samples were downloaded for Cuyahoga 2024, Cuyahoga May 2026 and Montgomery May 2026. They contain respectively 967,897 and 381 unique precinct UUIDs with no blank precinct IDs. Actual ballot-item arrays have 105,813 and 469 items, although metadata `ballotItemCount` says 1 in all three. Do not use that scalar to assert that a returned contest list is complete. [Vendor schema checks](data/results-sources/enhanced-schema-checks.json) preserve actual arrays, Senate identities, methods and clocks. Metadata distinguishes `isPrimary`, `isProduction`, `isOfficialResults`, `asOf`, `lastUpdated`, reporting status and optional `nextUpdateAt`; the latter was null. In Cuyahoga 2024, `asOf` is September 2026 but `lastUpdated` is December 2024. Preserve both; a migration/publication clock is not necessarily a vote-count time.

The [actual 2024 Senate detail request](https://app.enhancedvoting.com/results/public/api/elections/Cuyahoga-County-OH/general11052024/data/ballot-item/01000000-43fe-722b-2a1c-08dce6f80f76) returned **967 breakdown rows**, matching `breakdownResultCount=967` and the full 967-ID precinct list. Summary, breakdown and method totals reconcile **internally** for all four vendor choices. The six historical counting groups are Provisional, Early In-Person, Election Day, Post VBM, Vote By Mail and Post Election Day. These are actual group fields, not inferred from precinct names. Precinct reporting status 3 is displayed by the app as Fully Reported; it does not prove that no later correction can occur. `underVoteCount`/`overVoteCount` are null in this sample, not zero.

## Current counting rules, calendar and remaining-ballot eligibility

The [current text-only November schedule](https://www.ohiosos.gov/elections/voting-schedule-text-only) confirms ordinary polls **6:30 a.m.–7:30 p.m. November 3**. [R.C.3501.32](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3501.32) lets voters already in line vote and contains a narrow island-polling-place exception when all registered voters have voted. Ohio’s [election-day statute](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5.20) uses Eastern Standard Time; the offline `America/New_York` conversion gives **11:30 UTC opening November 3 and 00:30 UTC close November 4**. November offsets are UTC−5, after the November 1 daylight-saving change. This does not assign a timezone to an undocumented vendor/PDF print string.

No later November extension or replacement order was found in the inspected current directive index, current voting schedule and bounded primary-source searches. That is an October observation. Recheck actual state/county notices and any court order on Election Day. Directive 2026-48 says further instructions will follow if polls are court-extended; ordinary 7:30 close does not guarantee the final ballot has been cast or the first result released.

The general sample ballot also presents a pending voter-ID constitutional amendment; its presence does not itself change the rules under which this election is administered.

Directive 2026-48 requires initial county uploads to show **zero precincts reporting for absentee results**. Votes can therefore be present while that field is 0%. Supplemental counted-ballot/UOCAVA/provisional figures follow the last precinct or shortly after and can lag candidate totals. The live page says its ballots-counted value populates only after completion of the county’s unofficial canvass. Neither 0 nor 100% reporting supplies vote completeness. The live site describes outstanding UOCAVA as **requested but not returned by poll close**; the current directive’s operational statistic says **issued but not yet counted**. ## Replacement, corrections, overlap and fallback

1. **Gate real ingestion on identity and test status.** Follow the current official landing again, require actual November 3/unexpired-term identity, the current full candidate set, production/non-test status, geography, stage and source status. Reject the present mock XML, county zero preparations, May primaries, placeholders, robot challenges and incomplete responses. Keep the source’s null/blank IDs; do not manufacture IDs for the two Totals rows or infer a candidate UUID from a ballot label.
2. **Replace cumulative snapshots, preserving decreases.** Key observations by publisher/election/contest/stage/county/native unit/method and source vintage. Choose one complete eligible snapshot per scope; never append cumulative totals as new batches. Statewide, state county, local county, method totals, precinct rows and summary/PDF/Excel products overlap. A fall in a count is a revision requiring an audit trail, not an impossible negative batch to discard. Franklin’s observed post-audit decrease and the localized Cuyahoga difference show why this matters.
3. **Separate clocks from changes and completion.** Store immutable original bytes, source clock strings, test/official flags, generated sequence, headers, UTC fetch and hashes. Do not turn generated timestamps into vote events or equate a fully reported precinct/100%/signature-page message with state certification. Test current count/candidate/unit coverage after every source reset, contest rename or schema change.
4. **Reconcile scope before fallback substitution.** If state and local candidates differ, retain both and compare county/method/unit coverage and status. Prefer certified same-scope controls for historical baselines; do not average disagreeing authorities. Preserve named write-ins separately from generic invalid/unresolved categories. Quarantine unexplained differences rather than change a source value to force agreement.
5. **Manual fallback uses the same authority.** Open the BOE via the official directory, follow its actual current summary/precinct file, save the original accessible file or dated visual record, and record election, contest, status, full names, units/methods and UTC observation. Use BOE contact details from the state directory when files or eligibility definitions are missing. A phone explanation or screenshot is a manual observation, not another additive vote source. Do not require an invented November vendor path or an untested private CSS endpoint.

Proposed polling/backoff settings belong in the coordinator’s implementation strategy. This audit verifies advertised refresh code and a legally prescribed future submission schedule; it does not promise exact observed source delivery, an always-accessible API, a correction-event stream or a precise vote remainder.
