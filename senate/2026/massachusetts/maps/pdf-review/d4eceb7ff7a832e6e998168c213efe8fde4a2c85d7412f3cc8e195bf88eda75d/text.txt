WHAT ARE PFAS AND WHY ARE THEY
A PROBLEM?

Per Massachusetts Department of Environmental
protection (MassDEP)
 • “Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) are a family
  of chemicals used since the 1950s to manufacture stain-
   resistant, water-resistant, and non-stick products. PFAS
  are widely used in common consumer products as
  coatings, on food packaging, outdoor clothing, carpets,
  leather goods, ski and snowboard waxes, and more”
 • PFAS are in a broad array of commercial and consumer
  products; they do not break down easily and many bio-
  accumulate within the food chain and in the body.
 • Studies indicate that exposure to sufficient levels of PFAS
  may cause a variety of negative health effects:                             0
   • Developmental;
   • Thyroid, liver, and kidneys;
   • Endocrine and immune systems.

      THE SCALE OF THE PROBLEM

C.) USCDC estimates that most Americans currently have PFAS in their blood.


  USEPA estimates that up to 80 million Americans PWSs currently contain PFAS
   at 10 ppt or greater.

  MassDEP testing shows that more than 100 PWSs in Massachusetts are
   currently above the PFAS6 MCL (including Easton).

  PFAS have been detected in plant and wildlife around the globe.

   lacks adequately tested disposal pathways.            G

  PFAS – PFAS IATF estimates annual direct healthcare cost of PFAS exposure to
   the American public at $37-59 billion.

  PFAS IATF RECOMMENDATIONS FOR MASSACHUSETTS*  l•
   Fund PFAS Detection and Remediation
J       • Establish a PFAS Remediation Fund to provide grants to municipalities for treatment costs.

    Support Environmental Justice Communities
        • Establish SRF loan forgiveness for EJ Communities

   Phase Out PFAS in Consumer Products
        • Require manufacturers to notify state of intentionally added PFAS by 2025
        • Require consumer product labeling for “Product Contains PFAS”
        • Ban the sale of consumer products w/ intentionally added PFAS by 2030.
   Expand PFAS Regulation
        • Define PFAS as a class of chemicals rather than legacy approach of naming (and therefore regulating)
         only specific PFAS.
    Encourage Private Well PFAS Testing and Remediation

    Support Firefighters and Departments
        • Fund AFFF takeback (Easton has used this)
        • Prohibit AFFF use for training                            0
   Address PFAS Contamination Accountability

    Enhance Public Awareness of PFAS
