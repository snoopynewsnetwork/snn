# Rhode Island November 2026 source and counting rules

Observed October 20, 2026 UTC. This is bounded primary-source research, with whole originals, effective-date text and conflicting older publications retained. It is not exhaustive clearance of later court orders or unpublished administrative changes. No legal eligibility or current native-80% approval is granted.

The [official 2026 public-law list](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Lawrevision/plshort/pl2026bil.htm) is necessary: several codified section pages and the currently linked recount guide still display older text. The list itself says its linked chapter versions remain subject to proofreading and correction. Complete enacted HTML preserves strike-through and insertion markup. `derived/legal/*.enacted.txt` removes explicitly struck text for reading; the original HTML controls. Introduced bills are not substituted for enactments.

| Issue | Applicable evidence and operational consequence |

| Election, hours and local time | November 3, 2026. [17-18-10](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-18/17-18-10.htm) prints 7 a.m. for 38 municipalities and 9 a.m. for New Shoreham; its noon New Shoreham exception concerns presidential preference primaries. [17-18-11](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-18/17-18-11.htm) closes voting at 8 p.m. and protects qualified voters already in line. These are local civil hours, distinct from UTC retrieval clocks. The actual Cranston November warrant separately confirms 7 a.m.–8 p.m. No result-feed publication instant follows from closing time. |
| Senate winner and tie | [17-4-6](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-4/17-4-6.htm) elects the person with the largest vote total. Preserve the complete candidate field and qualified write-ins rather than a two-party majority threshold. [17-4-7](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-4/17-4-7.htm) provides a new election on the fourth Tuesday after an election with no plurality, with new nominations ten days before it. This federal-officer remedy is distinct from state-legislative, municipal, presidential-elector or primary tie rules. U.S. Senate adjudication of its membership is not displaced by a source-research calculation. |
| Requested recount threshold | [17-19-37.1](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-19/17-19-37.1.htm), single-winner public office: the trailing candidate must be **less than** the applicable threshold: for total race votes at most 20,000, min(2%, 200 votes); 20,001–100,000, min(1%, 500); above 100,000, min(0.5%, 1,500). Denominator includes candidate votes and irregular ballots under 17-19-31, rather than registration, all ballots or just the two leaders. A candidate at 5% or less who fails the re-feed threshold may petition for memory-device rereading; qualifying after that reread permits a re-feed request. This is a requested process, not an automatic recount or an instruction to round a boundary into eligibility. Multi-seat denominators and ballot-question/nonpublic-office thresholds are separate. |
| Federal recount deadline | Enacted [2026 chapter 60](http://webserver.rilegislature.gov/PublicLaws/law26/law26060.htm) and [chapter 61](http://webserver.rilegislature.gov/PublicLaws/law26/law26061.htm), effective upon passage June 10, amend 17-19-37 to cover state **and federal** returns and allow nine days **after declaration**. The [2026 Secretary calendar](https://vote.sos.ri.gov/Forms/Elections/Guides/2026ElecCal.pdf), physical page 13, instead schedules November 12 and describes nine days after election. Preserve both trigger descriptions. The linked guide under a 2025 URL internally says Rev 11/2024 and prints seven days/4 p.m.; current Part 14 also retains seven days. The enacted statute supersedes that older timing for federal candidates; do not silently convert the calendar date into a different statutory trigger. Original signed/notarized requests and Board receipt requirements remain printed administrative instructions; no outreach or filing occurred. |
| Write-in recount and tabulation | [17-19-37.2](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-19/17-19-37.2.htm) separately requires the specified write-in minimum (10 or 1% of race votes, whichever is greater; its no-printed-candidate exception remains in the whole section) plus the statutory trailing-margin test. Enacted [chapters 105](http://webserver.rilegislature.gov/PublicLaws/law26/law26105.htm) and [136](http://webserver.rilegislature.gov/PublicLaws/law26/law26136.htm), June 18 effective upon passage, amend 17-19-31 and 17-22-7: images of irregular ballots must be published; local boards adjudicate when write-ins may affect the outcome, including valid recount margins; qualifying write-in candidates can obtain adjudication under a promulgated procedure; the Board publishes adjudicated and overall totals. Incorrect-position and nominated-candidate irregular ballots and primary exclusions remain separately stated. Neither published images nor a generic bucket establishes a complete eligible named-choice universe. |
| Protest versus recount | Current [Part 7](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/regulations/Part/410-20-00-7) requires a general-election protest by 4 p.m. on the seventh day, with specific factual/legal grounds. Primary protest timing is the following day, not a Senate general-election rule. The Board's complete April 2025 complaint/appeal form separately states 20 days for local-board appeals, seven days/4 p.m. for general protests and 90 days for complaints, with original signed/notarized receipt and no electronic filing. Part 22 covers local-board appeals. Do not transfer the nine-day recount amendment automatically to protests, appeals or complaints. |
| Ordinary mail ballot application and return | [17-20-2.1](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-20/17-20-2.1.htm) specifies 4 p.m. on the 21st day before the election for ordinary applications; it also preserves the application-postmark exception when received by the 18th day. The current calendar/form identifies October 13. That application exception is not a late ordinary voted-ballot extension. [17-20-16](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-20/17-20-16.htm) requires ordinary voted ballots at the state Board by poll closing, November 3 at 8 p.m. No ordinary postmark-only substitute is established. Cast/received/accepted/certified/tabulated/published are different stages. |
| UOCAVA and disabled voters | 17-20-16 provides the 17-20-6.1 exception: receipt at the state Board by 4 p.m. on the seventh day after an election (calendar November 10); its primary three-day provision is separate. Preserve the requirement that the ballot be cast on or before election day. [2026 chapter 3](http://webserver.rilegislature.gov/PublicLaws/law26/law26003.htm) and [chapter 4](http://webserver.rilegislature.gov/PublicLaws/law26/law26004.htm), effective March 31, amend the post-December-2025 UOCAVA electronic-transmission and disabled-voter provisions. The approved-system security-review/framework requirements and disabled-voter certification are retained in full. Expired December 2025 subsections are not treated as operative 2026 provisions. No account, ballot-return service or individual voter lookup was accessed. |
| Early and emergency | [17-20-2.2](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-20/17-20-2.2.htm) provides the 20-day emergency period and 4 p.m. day-before application cutoff. Current [Part 29](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/regulations/Part/410-20-00-29), effective July 7, 2026, defines early voting as emergency mail voting, requires at least one municipal site during normal hours, and preserves its holiday exception and permitted extended/weekend hours. For this general election the Board/calendar advertise October 14 through November 2 at 4 p.m. The whole 39-row early-hours table preserves rooms, blank room fields, addresses, weekday hours, notes and literal apparent typos. They are not election-day hours or reporting methods by themselves. Part 29 prohibits printing vote totals during early voting and requires public closing/sealing/results tapes after election-night poll closing. |
| Mail processing, IDs and cure | [Current Part 16](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/regulations/Part/410-20-00-16) allows machine processing up to 20 days before election day, with totals no earlier than 8 p.m. The scanned older mail-tabulation guide still says 9 p.m. and has a former address; it is retained as a historical publication, not the operative clock. [2026 chapters 418](http://webserver.rilegislature.gov/PublicLaws/law26/law26418.htm) and [419](http://webserver.rilegislature.gov/PublicLaws/law26/law26419.htm), June 23 effective upon passage, update ordinary/emergency application identifiers, the secure observation area and signature-review identifiers. State staff compare CVRS/application signatures in the specified review stages, can consider supplied ID/SSN identifiers, segregate unresolved discrepancies, notify voters and make final determinations. [Current Part 23](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/regulations/Part/410-20-00-23) provides deficiency/omission and signature-discrepancy cure with a Deficient Mail Ballot Attestation Form; general-election cure must be **received**, not merely postmarked, by 4 p.m. seven days later. Calendar November 10 matches. Old registry versions with older affidavit names are not substituted for the downloaded current rule. |
| Provisional and in-person ID | [17-19-24.2](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-19/17-19-24.2.htm) specifies acceptable photo identification (unexpired or expired no more than six months) and permits a provisional ballot if it is missing. [17-19-24.3](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-19/17-19-24.3.htm) makes the local signature examination relevant to that ballot's validity. [Part 13](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/regulations/Part/410-20-00-13) retains full/federal-only/disqualified dispositions, local certification and state tabulation, including addition to election-day precinct results. First-time mail-registration ID requirements and their printed deadlines are a separate category, not the seven-day mail-signature cure. Personal provisional-status lookup is not a public aggregate remaining-vote feed and was not queried. |
| Canvass, certification, corrections | [17-7-5](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-7/17-7-5.htm) supplies state Board supervisory/recount authority; [17-8-5](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-8/17-8-5.htm) supplies local duties. [17-19-32](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-19/17-19-32.htm) and 33 require signed returns, posted copies, sealing and transmission. [Chapter 22](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-22/INDEX.htm) separates tabulation, public sessions, investigation of local returns, certificates and recorded totals; 17-22-5.1 permits an aggrieved candidate's pre-certification investigation request. The Board can resolve discrepancies before certification; an HTTP label or numbers are not a separately acquired signed certification instrument. Current Part 28 and 17-19-37.4 address audits; an audit is distinct from a recount and unofficial publication. |
| Later-effective authority change | [2026 chapters 420](http://webserver.rilegislature.gov/PublicLaws/law26/law26420.htm) and [421](http://webserver.rilegislature.gov/PublicLaws/law26/law26421.htm) amend municipal vacancy procedures and chief local election official duties but explicitly take effect **January 1, 2027**. Their CVRS-access administrative provisions are recorded only; no administrative account or operation was attempted. They are not applied to the November 2026 authority chain. |
