                                       Cook County Clerk




Dear Friends:

The Cook County Clerk’s Office is proud to present our signature Post-Election Report, a
comprehensive tabulation and breakdown of election data that also includes analysis of the
voting trends that shaped the 2024 Presidential General Election.

While the overall number of ballots cast in the November 5, 2024 election was very close to
vote totals in the 2016 and 2020 Presidential contests, turnout percentage for suburban Cook
County was at a more than 20-year low. The 66 percent turnout rate represents a drop off
in turnout levels that ranged from 71 to 74 percent in the previous six presidential election
cycles, a decrease likely attributed to a shift in voter engagement, political polarization, or
voter apathy.

The number of registered voters in the 2024 General Election decreased slightly compared to
the 2020 General Election. However, the overall number of registered voters in suburban Cook
County has remained steady and continues to trend upward with more than 1.64 million voters
currently registered.

Cook County voters returned to more traditional voting methods in this election, with a
notable 57 percent increase in in-person voting on Election Day. Early Voting increased
marginally by 3.7 percent. Conversely, Mail Voting saw a significant decrease of 58.2 percent, a
drop that can be largely attributed to the extraordinary surge in the use of mail ballots during
the 2020 Covid Pandemic.

The 2024 Presidential Election also ushered in a change in leadership here at the Cook County
Clerk’s Office with my election to the position of Clerk to fill the vacancy of the late Clerk
Karen A. Yarbrough. This is not a privilege that I take lightly, and I am committed to building
on the strong foundation that has been laid to lead an office that prioritizes professionalism,
equity, transparency, and efficiency.

This 2024 Presidential Post-Election Report has been assembled
to provide a comprehensive look at voting totals, trends, and
statistics throughout suburban Cook County. As always, this
report, full election results, and printable maps are available at
cookcountyclerk.com/elections.

Yours in Democracy,




Monica Gordon
Cook County Clerk

  2   Post-Election Report 2024
                Declines in Registration and Turnout in 2024
Though the number of ballots cast in the November 5, 2024 Presidential Election – 1,089,942 – was the second
highest total in the past 24 years, the turnout percentage was at a more than twenty-year low at 66 percent for
suburban Cook County.

This marks a significant decline from the consistent turnout levels of around 71-74 percent observed in the previous
six election cycles, highlighting a potential shift in voter engagement, political polarization, or voter apathy.

This drop – despite registration options and ballot access expansion over recent years – stands out even more when
compared to the 2004 and 2008 elections, which had marginally lower numbers of registered voters but maintained
higher turnout rates.

  4   Post-Election Report 2024
                             Pre-Election Day Voting Patterns Shift
Pre-Election Day voting in all its forms – Early Voting, Mail Voting, and Grace Period Registration and Voting – accounted
for 60.4 percent of all ballots cast, a decrease of 21.5 percent from the November 2020 Presidential Election.

While Early Voting increased marginally by 3.7 percent, Mail Voting saw a significant drop of 58.2 percent. This decline
is largely attributable to the extraordinary surge in mail ballots during the 2020 coronavirus pandemic, which heavily
influenced voting patterns during that year’s presidential contest.

The reduction in Mail Voting coincided with a nearly identical percentage swing in the increased number of voters casting
ballots on Election Day, which rose by 57 percent. Additionally, Election Day saw a record-breaking 19,195 residents
registering to vote in suburban Cook County, further emphasizing a shift in voting behaviors.

While the majority of voters still opted to cast their ballots prior to Election Day, this year’s data suggests a notable return
to more traditional voting habits. This trend likely reflects the diminishing impact of pandemic-era policies and concerns.

voters in the 7 months since the March 19 Presidential Primary.

Various outreach efforts helped add eligible voters to the rolls, one of which was Election Day Registration. The
program allowed 19,195 eligible voters to both register and vote in their home precinct on Election Day.

high-participation township for the previous five presidential elections. River Forest Township followed closely with a
turnout of 78.1 percent. The townships with the next highest turnout were New Trier at 77.4 percent and Lemont at
77.1 percent. All four townships have consistently demonstrated strong voter engagement, reflecting stable trends
over recent election cycles.
