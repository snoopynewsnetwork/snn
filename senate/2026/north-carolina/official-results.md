# North Carolina official results publication channels

Inspected **2026-10-18 UTC / 2026-10-17 America/Chicago**. This is a source investigation with saved responses and offline checks. It does not implement a live collector, measure election-night lag, approve boundaries, or calibrate probabilities.

The [NCSBE results page](https://www.ncsbe.gov/results-data/election-results) links the [official dashboard](https://er.ncsbe.gov/). Its public files provide statewide, county, candidate, voting-method and reporting-unit observations. The [NCSBE county-board directory](https://vt.ncsbe.gov/BOEInfo/) supplies the local authority chain for **all 100 counties**. The November dashboard and its linked ordinary media ZIP are actually available, but the inspected candidate counts are **all zero setup data**, not votes counted. No explicit test flag was found; zero setup is the evidence-supported classification.

[Source inventory](data/official-results-sources.csv): **100 county rows and four shared state-channel rows**, with native NC IDs, FIPS, exact directory URLs, fetch outcomes, timestamps, local discoveries, evidence IDs, overlap, precedence and gaps. [Coverage by geography](data/results-sources/coverage.csv) retains every county in statewide arithmetic. The reconciled priority is exactly the **38 counties below**, supplying **4,576,486 / 5,699,145 = 80.30127326%** of certified 2024 presidential votes; the top 15 are contained. Haywood remains an additional midterm sensitivity geography, with full state-source coverage, but is outside the primary deep-inspection set. NC had no 2024 Senate race: **2024 president** and **2022 Senate** remain separate throughout. See [turnout](turnout.md) and [partisanship](county-partisanship.md).

## November rules, time and certification

**General-election Senate winner: highest vote total (plurality).** Current [G.S. 163-182.15(d)](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_163/GS_163-182.15.html), downloaded in this assignment, directs that general-election candidates with the highest number of votes be declared elected. The current [upcoming-election page](https://www.ncsbe.gov/voting/upcoming-election) identifies the November 3, 2026 statewide general election and one U.S. Senate seat. Thus the general election does not require a majority threshold or the primary's 30% nomination rule. Count every candidate; the historical two-party break-even scenario is a conditional comparison, not the legal all-candidate winning test. Ties/protests/recounts require their separate official procedures.

**Ordinary poll hours are 6:30 a.m.–7:30 p.m. Eastern; November 3 close converts to 2026-11-04 00:30 UTC.** The current upcoming-election page confirms the local hours. [G.S. 163-166.25](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_163/GS_163-166.25.html) permits voters already in line at closing to vote; it also permits extensions for qualifying opening delays/interruption and describes provisional treatment for voting under extended-hour orders. The state's [Digital Commons timezone explanation](https://digitalcommons.nc.gov/guide/help-topics/how-find-and-use-your-sitemap) explicitly identifies NC's Eastern offsets; the inspected [49 CFR 71.5 boundary](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-A/part-71/section-71.5) places the Eastern/Central boundary west of NC. The UTC conversion is calculated using `America/New_York`, whose November 3 offset is −05:00. This timezone evidence does **not** assign a timezone to the dashboard's undocumented timestamp strings.

No November extension order was identified in the inspected [upcoming-election page](https://www.ncsbe.gov/voting/upcoming-election), [reporting timeline](https://www.ncsbe.gov/results-data/election-results/election-night-reporting-timeline) and [current press-release index](https://www.ncsbe.gov/news/recent-press-releases). That bounded October observation cannot establish that no later order will occur. Recheck State Board orders and county notices on election day. NCSBE's timeline expressly says significant extensions can alter reporting times.

**Scheduled county canvass: November 13, 2026, 11 a.m.; scheduled state canvass: November 24, 2026, 11 a.m.** These exact dates appear on physical/printed page 16 of the downloaded [2025–26 statewide candidate guide](https://s3.amazonaws.com/dl.ncsbe.gov/Candidate%20Filing/2025-26_Statewide_Candidate_Guide.pdf), verified in the [saved page image](data/results-sources/candidate-guide-page16.png). Current [G.S. 163-182.5](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_163/GS_163-182.5.html) establishes the tenth-day county meeting and Tuesday-three-weeks-after-election state meeting, with statutory provisions for delayed completion/adjournment. [G.S. 163-182.4](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/ByChapter/Chapter_163.html) places national offices under State Board jurisdiction. The guide's provisional-ballot paragraph also contains inconsistent “third day” wording; do not substitute that phrase for the explicit November dates and current statute.

These are **future scheduled meetings, not completed canvasses or certified November results**. The [post-election procedures page](https://www.ncsbe.gov/about-elections/election-security/post-election-procedures-and-audits) distinguishes unofficial election-night reporting, eligible ballots processed during canvass and authentication. The observed November dashboard title itself says `UNOFFICIAL GENERAL ELECTION RESULTS`. Store publication status separately from the presence of a date or a 100%-reporting field.

The dashboard constructs its data path from the **actually downloaded catalog election date** and actual county IDs. Its contest-link renderer constructs the details page; that page exposes the precinct-file loader. The dashboard's navigation links `downloads.html`; that downloaded app constructs the media ZIP URL from the same catalog. These are inspected application-generated routes, all actually requested, not predictions of future paths. These are different products/vintages; do not assume a synchronized release. The media file has no row-level vote-publication timestamp or release ID. Pin snapshots, retain timestamps and compare schemas/candidate sets rather than silently combine versions. ## Schema and completion semantics

| Resource | Native fields and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| County JSON | `cid` native county ID, `cnm` name, `tle` election/status title; `prt/ptl/ppt` reported/total/percent precincts; `bct` ballots cast; `rtl` registered voters; `bpt` registered-voter participation; `imp/sub` displayed as Last County Upload/Submit in app code. Keep literal timestamps and a separate UTC retrieval. |
| Result JSON | `cid`, `gid` group, `lid` local contest, `cnm`, `bnm` ballot name, `pty`, `vfr`, `vct` total; **`evc` Election Day**, **`ovc` early/One Stop**, `avc` absentee by mail, `pvc` provisional; `prt/ptl` completion units; `pct` fractional share (e.g. `0.5086`). Acronyms alone are misleading: `evc` is not early voting. |
| Precinct JSON | `lid`, `aid` native reporting-unit ID, `anm` display name, `sta` status text, `bnm`, count fields, display colors; `pct` is a formatted percentage string (e.g. `67.01%`), unlike summary `pct`. No numeric candidate ID was established; preserve ballot labels, party, contest and source vintage together. |
| Ordinary media TSV | Native precinct string plus real/administrative flag; voting-method counts are columns, not mutually exclusive source files. Join county-native name/ID to the exact FIPS crosswalk, then election/group/unit/choice. Administrative rows must remain in totals even if they have no polygon. |

The complete saved [2024 Wake detail file](https://er.ncsbe.gov/enr/20241105/data/precinct_92.txt) contains **2,133 presidential rows = nine choices × 237 units**. All candidate/method sums reproduce the county summary, after **two explicit label aliases only**: detail `Claudia De la Cruz` and `Shiva Ayyadurai` correspond to summary names with `(Write-In)`. Raw labels are unchanged. [Unit extract](data/results-sources/wake-2024-president-units.csv) retains both raw and canonical labels. Physical-unit rows have `Final Canvass Results (official)`; the 25 administrative-unit rows per candidate have blank status. A blank administrative status is not a missing vote or proof of an unfinished count.

Wake dashboard completion is **212/212** in 2024, while the local summary and detailed file cover **237 units**, including `ABSEN`, `EVAB`, `EVBOE`, `PROVI`, `TRANS` and other early-site IDs. Its 2022 local summary counts **280** units while the dashboard counts **208/208** precincts. Counts refer to different unit universes. A unit marked reported/official can coexist with later eligible-ballot processing and corrections. Do not calculate vote completion, uncounted votes, or registration-minus-ballots from these ratios.

The **original directory-linked** county requests returned **81 HTTP 200, 14 HTTP 403, two HTTP 404, one HTTP 520, and two transport failures**. These are fetch outcomes, not 81 verified result feeds. The failures include Johnston (connection) and Bertie (DNS). HTTPS/current-domain follow-ups recovered Johnston, Catawba and Craven. Web-rendered follow-ups exposed official Buncombe, Cabarrus, Onslow, Henderson, Franklin and Rockingham navigation despite raw failures; Durham/Wilson remained blocked and Chatham's current result bytes were not retrieved. Preserve the differing access paths, not a single misleading “works” flag.

Outside the 38, the directory failures were **Granville, Sampson, Dare, Person, Bertie, Mitchell and Hyde**. Each remains in the inventory and exact historical/state media coverage; their deeper local channels are untested. [Coverage CSV](data/results-sources/coverage.csv) names the local gap for every geography. Some accessible priority pages have no verified candidate-results destination: **New Hanover, Robeson, Burke and Caldwell**. Their election notices/participation reports must not be promoted into candidate feeds.

Four resources were fetched twice under distinct evidence IDs: November county JSON, STATE results JSON, media ZIP and outstanding ZIP. The first two bodies were unchanged over **about 210 seconds**, media over **about 183 seconds**, and outstanding over **about 44 seconds**. Exact timestamps/hashes are in [observed-stability.json](data/results-sources/observed-stability.json). This proves bounded stability only. No changing election-night snapshot, correction sequence, lag distribution, conditional-GET behavior or rate-limit policy was measured.

The [dashboard documentation](https://www.ncsbe.gov/results-data/election-results/about-election-results-dashboard) states **5–10-minute updates** after polls close; the [timeline](https://www.ncsbe.gov/results-data/election-results/election-night-reporting-timeline) gives approximate mail, early and precinct windows and warns about county variation/extensions. These are publisher expectations. Client refresh/cache-buster code, carousel scroll delays and repeated zero setup downloads do not establish a measured publication guarantee.

Recommended source precedence for coordinator implementation:

1. Historical comparisons use certified state/county abstracts, including the already resolved 2024 baseline selection. The dashboard's `OFFICIAL` label does not erase its observed disagreement with those statements.
2. For proposed live observations, start with one coherent state county/candidate/method snapshot, retaining its full provenance. The ordinary media file and dashboard overlap. The state `cid=0` total is a cross-check, not another batch to add to the county sums.
3. A verified newer county publication or correction can supersede the matching observation after election, candidate universe, method, status and vintage checks. Preserve the old state value, the county value, reason and replacement time. No unconditional rule that a local site is always earlier is supported.
4. State links on local sites, scrolling variants, local PDFs, county totals and their precincts are overlapping observations. Municipal totals are subsets. Never add or average them. News/manual substitutions remain explicit and are governed by the coordinator's separate strategy.

Snapshots are cumulative. **Replace the prior compatible observation; retain signed differences, including decreases.** Retain source bytes, election/date/group/local contest, county/native unit, candidate raw label/party, methods, source status, source time literal/timezone if known, HTTP metadata, retrieval UTC and parser version. Missing fields/blocked sources are null, not zero. A release can reclassify a write-in or correct a total; never enforce monotonic growth. Actual downward revisions were not replayed in this assignment, so that behavior still needs a genuine historical/live rehearsal.

## Reproduction and unresolved gates

Run from the repository root; Python 3 plus curl and Poppler are sufficient. These scripts write only the assigned results paths:

```sh
# Offline checks/build from immutable retained responses and read-only turnout inputs.
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 senate/2026/north-carolina/data/results-sources/build.py
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 senate/2026/north-carolina/data/results-sources/check_local_pdfs.py
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 senate/2026/north-carolina/data/results-sources/validate.py

# Explicit public URL capture, only when a new observation is intentionally wanted.
python3 senate/2026/north-carolina/data/results-sources/collect.py NEW_SOURCE_ID ACTUAL_OBSERVED_URL
```

`collect.py` reuses existing IDs; use a new ID for a changed observation. `--refresh` intentionally overwrites that ID and is unsuitable for preserving a time series. Responses are decoded by `curl --compressed`, then stored losslessly as gzip; metadata hashes refer to the decoded original body. Headers remain separate. Identical bodies may share one stored blob, with every fetch retaining separate metadata. HTML text/link JSON and web-tool excerpts are labeled derivatives. No authenticated or undocumented private API was used. The largest decoded response retained is the complete 6,021,367-byte historical Wake precinct JSON; the media ZIP's larger uncompressed TSV is inside a small downloaded ZIP. Each stored object is below 40 MB.

[Validation](data/results-sources/validation.json) checks all 100 IDs/FIPS, the exact 38-county set/volume, every historical candidate/method sum, ordinary-ZIP agreement, the ten certified differences, the detailed Wake totals, all current setup counts, county real-unit denominators and all 100 outstanding rows. [Local PDF checks](data/results-sources/local-summary-checks.json), [artifact checks](data/results-sources/artifact-checks.json) and [SHA-256 manifest](data/results-sources/SHA256SUMS) cover the additional samples, links, hashes and bounds. Read-only dependencies on completed turnout artifacts are explicit in the source manifest; none of those files was edited.

Remaining operational gates are concrete: genuine November changing snapshots and source lag; coherent release/version detection across dashboard/media; source timestamp timezones; county-page access and local destinations listed above; independently verified outstanding-ballot semantics and the 71-county spoiled-field conflict; current provisional product content; any November reporting-unit/boundary crosswalk and administrative units without polygons; and observed correction/recount/certification transitions.
