# North Carolina live collection and break-even strategy

Research snapshot **2026-10-18 UTC / 2026-10-17 Central**. This is an operating proposal supported by bounded source inspections. No continuously running collector or calibrated live Needle has been implemented. See the [official inventory](official-results.md), [news survey](local-news-results.md), [turnout](turnout.md) and [historical targets](county-partisanship.md).

## Source and geographic plan

Collect all **100 counties**. The measured turnout report defines the exact priority set for deeper inspection and map assets; every county remains in statewide arithmetic. NC had no 2024 U.S. Senate contest: use 2024 president for recent volume and composition comparison and 2022 Senate for the recent Senate/midterm baseline. Candidate effects are separate from turnout mix.

Start with the state-linked [NCSBE dashboard](https://www.ncsbe.gov/results-data/election-results) and publicly linked results files verified in the inventory. Use county board channels for authoritative local explanations and any independently published detailed returns. Do not assume 100 separate live vendors are needed where a state file already covers the counties. Establish a field-level precedence rule for each geographic unit: coherent official state county record by default, a verified county publication when it demonstrably supersedes or explains that record, then explicitly attributed manual/news fallback. Keep disagreement visible; never sum overlapping state, county and municipal totals.

The inspection actually retrieved the November [county summary](https://er.ncsbe.gov/enr/20261103/data/county.txt), [state results](https://er.ncsbe.gov/enr/20261103/data/results_0.txt), all 38 priority-county result files and the [ordinary precinct media ZIP](https://dl.ncsbe.gov/ENRS/2026_11_03/results_pct_20261103.zip). They are **zero-vote setup snapshots**, not election returns. The setup Senate group is `1383`; preserve its election and contest context and verify the final ballot configuration before ingestion. The media file covers all 100 counties, with 2,626 physical and 216 administrative reporting units. Its publication metadata differs from the dashboard's; they are not one synchronized release. Repeated unchanged samples establish bounded stability only.

The [NCSBE dashboard documentation](https://www.ncsbe.gov/results-data/election-results/about-election-results-dashboard) describes 5–10-minute updates and tables by county and voting method. Its [timeline](https://www.ncsbe.gov/results-data/election-results/election-night-reporting-timeline) describes approximate mail reporting after 7:30 p.m., early voting later in the evening, and precinct reporting through midnight. Those are published expectations, not a measured promise for every county. Verify extensions on election day and do not treat an expected time as a missing-results error.

## Proposed collection intervals

| Channel | Initial request/check interval | Admission requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified state media/download file | 5 minutes, with jitter | Confirm November election and Senate contest; validate candidate labels/party and native geography before parsing votes. |
| Priority-county source with a verified distinct publication | 2–5 minutes | Honor source limits; establish actual change rate before increasing requests. |
| Other county landing/PDF or manual lane | 5–10 minutes while relevant | Record document/status/time; avoid repeated downloads of unchanged files. |
| NYT and regional scoreboard checks | 5 minutes if permitted and verified, otherwise timestamped manual checks | Real November contest, compatible geography and known supplier; never ingest test or historical primary votes. |
| Canvass-period checks | 30–60 minutes during announced board activity, otherwise daily | Follow the board's posted meeting/publication schedule. |

These are proposed request intervals. NCSBE's documented 5–10 minutes concerns its dashboard refresh; WLOS's 30-second JavaScript and WWAY's historical five-minute text concern different products. None establishes a faster flow of new official votes. Use conditional requests where supported, per-host concurrency limits, timeouts, jitter and exponential backoff. Separate last successful fetch from last changed result. A stable count is not itself a source outage.

## Snapshots, normalization and corrections

Before parsing, store immutable response bytes, URL, UTC retrieval, HTTP metadata, hash, election/contest identifiers, source publication time with timezone, parser version and unofficial/canvassed/certified status. Retain each changed snapshot and a fetch log. Reject challenge HTML, app shells, empty/test data and wrong elections even when HTTP status is 200. Keep the last coherent observation and display stale/missing coverage.

Use county FIPS plus source-native county, precinct/administrative-unit and voting-method IDs. Retain original labels and unmatched rows. Administrative early-vote, absentee and provisional units may not have physical precinct polygons. Do not spatially distribute their totals without a defensible crosswalk. Current map approval and baseline-to-2026 crosswalks require separate verification.

No stable numeric candidate identifier was established. Keep source labels, party, contest and election vintage in candidate keys and review aliases explicitly. In the tested JSON, `evc` is **Election Day**, `ovc` is **early/One Stop**, `avc` is mail and `pvc` is provisional; do not infer meanings from abbreviations. `pct` can be a fraction in one product and a formatted string in another. Upload/submission literals lack a confirmed timezone. County `prt/ptl` counts reporting units; they do not measure votes remaining. The two download labels pointing at the same ordinary ZIP are one source.

The [map package](precinct-maps.md) retains 2024-07-23 and 2026-08-24 published snapshots for the exact 38-county set: 76 GeoJSONs and 76 previews. These are downloaded published boundaries, with provenance and diagnostics. November legal approval and a complete results crosswalk remain unverified. Administrative units, unmatched physical labels, changed IDs, one retained invalid historical polygon and overlap diagnostics prohibit an automatic one-to-one allocation of all votes to those polygons.

NCSBE's [historical-file documentation](https://www.ncsbe.gov/results-data/election-results/historical-election-results-data) distinguishes ordinary results from post-election **precinct-sorted** datasets with statistical noise. Use original results/certified publications for exact total reconciliation. Noisy precinct-sorted counts must not become official live counts or be silently forced to reconcile. A geography-compatible modeling use needs an explicit uncertainty and source label.

Snapshots are cumulative: replace the observation for the same unit and source, then derive changes between compatible versions. Retain decreases and candidate reassignment corrections. Check per-method sums against totals, county sums against the statewide candidate view, candidate universe and document revision. Never combine mismatched release versions into an apparently complete state. A county appearing under a new unit label requires review rather than adding it as new votes.

## Counted and outstanding votes

Keep observed candidate votes fixed. Estimate only remaining votes, with low/base/high ranges and the source/time of each assumption. The actual November [aggregate outstanding file](https://s3.amazonaws.com/dl.ncsbe.gov/ENRS/2026_11_03/absentee_spoiled_outstanding_20261103.zip) was retrieved and checked for all 100 counties: sent 143,606; accepted 38,636; received but not counted 1,611; outstanding 103,359; spoiled early votes 3,162. It has file publication metadata but no row-level as-of time. The documented inclusion relationship between spoiled early votes and received-but-not-counted fails in **71 counties** in this snapshot. Preserve the raw values and flag that unresolved conflict; do not repair or add those categories into an exact remaining-vote total. The [publisher summary](https://www.ncsbe.gov/results-data/absentee-and-provisional-data/outstanding-ballots-data-report-summary) describes daily publication, not an observed cadence. The November provisional product was not tested. Request/return/acceptance records are not candidate votes; do not infer party choice from registration or count already-tabulated ballots twice. Only aggregate evidence was downloaded for this investigation.

The [2025–26 official candidate guide](https://s3.amazonaws.com/dl.ncsbe.gov/Candidate%20Filing/2025-26_Statewide_Candidate_Guide.pdf) and [canvass statute](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_163/GS_163-182.5.html) establish county canvass November 13 and state canvass November 24, 2026, at 11 a.m. The guide's generic provisional paragraph is inconsistent with its explicit calendar; retain that discrepancy and use the verified current dates/statute. Eligible cured, provisional and military/overseas ballots may be handled after election night. Election-night precinct completion cannot certify final votes. The [post-election procedures page](https://www.ncsbe.gov/about-elections/election-security/post-election-procedures-and-audits) provides the audit context.

The verified general-election rule is [plurality](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_163/GS_163-182.15.html), so retain all four setup candidates rather than treating D/R totals as the entire contest. Ordinary polling hours are 6:30 a.m.–7:30 p.m. Eastern on November 3; the ordinary close is **2026-11-04 00:30 UTC** after the daylight-saving transition. Check actual [extensions](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_163/GS_163-166.25.html) before declaring voting complete.

Compare incoming returns to the same county, method and boundary vintage in the historical baseline. An early-vote-heavy first batch is not a random county sample. Where allocation to comparable units is unresolved, show coarser comparisons and wider uncertainty. Prioritize counties contributing the most remaining uncertainty as the night develops, alongside the historical volume priority set.

With counted statewide D-minus-R lead `L` and positive expected remaining major-party votes `U`, the conditional statewide tie threshold is `(1-L/U)/2` Democratic share of the remainder. For selected county `c`, use `(U_c-L-sum(other remaining net votes))/(2*U_c)` only when `U_c>0`. Show values below 0% as already secured under the assumptions and above 100% as impossible under them. Include third-candidate possibilities separately; these equations are two-candidate scenarios, not probabilities.

## Rehearsal and manual operation

Before polls close, pin the actual November ballot/contest configuration from official links; confirm native identifiers, method schemas, source status, timezone and any voting-hour extensions. Save a zero/scaffold sample separately. Replay saved genuine updates to prove idempotence, correction handling, overlapping source replacement, stale-source display and missing-is-not-zero behavior. A manual operator records source URL, screenshot/document or bytes, publisher and observation times, exact geography, candidates, values and reason for substitution.

The coverage board must show all 100 counties, last fetch/change, available method/precinct detail, counted votes, unknown/unmatched units and outstanding-vote range. Keep external race calls and NYT/other forecasts separate. The first implementation milestone is reliable collection plus deterministic break-even scenarios. Probabilities require a dated prior, genuine timestamped historical replay, held-out calibration and interval/error checks under the [root plan](../../../ELECTION_NIGHT_PLAN.md). Synthetic report orders cannot establish calibration.
