### Winner, tie, failure, recount and contest

State Constitution Article VII §5 provides highest-vote/plurality election, with its express exceptions; the municipal runoff exception is not applied to U.S. Senate. NMSA 1-15-12/13 expressly governs Senate election/canvass. Section 1-13-11 directs resolution of an election tie by lot using its specified committee; the separate Article V §2 legislative tie procedure for listed state executive offices is not silently substituted for Senate. These are state election/certification procedures: **U.S. Constitution Article I §5 separately makes the Senate judge of its members' elections, returns and qualifications**. Neither a state certificate nor this research decides a federal chamber contest.

Section 1-13-24(A) prevents certification of an **unopposed general-election write-in** below 2% of the preceding gubernatorial vote in the relevant jurisdiction. Its primary, local-election and U.S. House-vacancy subsections have different scopes. This threshold is not applied to an observed opposed 2026 Senate contest. Section 1-15-14 and the Seventeenth Amendment address Senate vacancies; a local top-two runoff or presidential-elector procedure does not become a Senate failure-of-election rule. An unresolved contest/qualification, a tie and an unopposed write-in failure are kept separate rather than assigning an invented Senate runoff date.

For Senate, 1-14-24(A)(1) requires an automatic recount when the top-two margin is **strictly less than 0.25% of total votes cast for the office**. Its five-or-fewer-votes provision belongs to the other-office category, not federal Senate. For a multicounty office, SOS gives notice after the state canvass and the state canvassing board orders the recount. The replacement 1.10.23 rule governs the actual procedures; primary/local thresholds are not imported.

A candidate-requested recount/recheck under 1-14-14 is applied for within six days after completion of the proper canvass, with applications to SOS. Sections 1-14-15/18 address deposits/costs and conduct; the complete texts are preserved without treating every application as an automatic recount. A statutory election contest is a separate verified complaint under 1-14-1/3, filed within 30 days after issuance of the successful candidate's certificate. The retained annotations distinguish historical interpretations from the current text. No exact future litigation deadline hour or case outcome is invented.

### Voting, receipt, processing and cure

NMSA 1-12-1 prints **7:00 a.m.–7:00 p.m. Election Day**; 1-12-25.1/25.2 and related provisions handle provisional eligibility rather than turning an identification issue into an automatic final rejection. Required identification under 1-1-24 includes its statutory non-photo route; a universal photo-ID claim would be wrong. Early-voting 1-6-5.7 separately requires the prescribed identification and offers a provisional ballot when it is not supplied. Same-day-registration provisions and their particular documentation remain distinct from ordinary in-person identification and the primary-only party rules.

Election Day is November 3, 2026. The local current pages and state source clocks use Mountain-time context (`MT` is printed on ENR); the San Juan client explicitly uses America/Denver for its HTTP clock. Attempts to retrieve complete current DOT/eCFR time-zone authority returned access failures/challenge material. Thus this package does not claim independently exhaustive current federal boundary/DST verification. The [date calculations](data/results-sources/legal-calculations.json) show the conditional America/Denver conversion of a 7 PM November 3 close to 02:00 UTC November 4; that is a labelled calculation, **not a source-printed UTC hour**. Timezone-less publisher timestamps remain unknown. No regional time-zone split is inferred from publisher clocks.

Ordinary mailed applications under 1-6-5(A) are due 14 days before the election: **October 20**, derived without an invented closing hour. Mailing/early clerk voting begins 28 days before: **October 6**. Alternate early voting begins the third preceding Saturday, **October 17**, through **October 31**. Section 1-6-5.7 gives regular clerk-business hours and a permitted extra-hours range, while each county sets actual site hours; its 7 AM–9 PM outer range is not every site's schedule. The source's current local guidance and the statutory date arithmetic are preserved separately.

Section 1-6-10(A) requires completed mailed envelopes to be received at the clerk, a polling place, post office retrieval or monitored secured container by **7 PM Election Day**. It supplies no ordinary postmark grace period. Later envelopes are retained unopened and late-category counts updated; a late-ballot administrative update is not a late vote admission. Section 1-6B-8 applies the same receipt deadline to military/overseas ballots. It permits direct secured electronic return by a federal qualified elector with the required secrecy-waiver affidavit. Section 1-6B-7's 45-day transmission rule derives **September 19** for this election; that is distinct from a voted-ballot return extension. Accessible electronic delivery under other provisions does not itself authorize every voter to return a ballot electronically.

The return envelope verification in 1-6-10 checks **signature presence and matching last four SSN digits**, with notice and cure for missing/incorrect information; it is not a graphological signature-match rule. Additional federal first-time-mail-registration identification requirements have their separate statutory scope. Section 1-6-10(F) permits missing/corrected information through the conclusion of the appeal process and expressly provides different handling before/after board convening, adjournment and county approval. Sections 1-6-14 and 1-12-25.3/25.4, current 1.10.22.11 and 1.10.15 preserve provisional/rejected-ballot review and appeal/correction procedures. The rule's Friday-before-state-canvass information/documentation endpoint calculates to **November 20** if the statutory November 24 state meeting applies. This is not an invented statewide November 6 cure cutoff, nor a source-printed hour. Friday-following-election rejection handling in 1-6-14 is a different step.

The SOS absentee guidance's “mail on or before” November 3 wording does not create postmark acceptance beyond its same-page 7 PM receipt requirement. Its delivery-person guidance is narrower than the current statute's household/caregiver/continuing-personal-relationship language. Harding's current local “last mail” wording is also separate from the October 20 statutory application deadline. Original disagreements survive; the current operative statute controls the legal description.

Section 1-6-14 allows absent-voter-board processing before Election Day, with distinct timing for fewer than 10,000 versus at least 10,000 mailed ballots. Early work does not authorize early public results: counting/generation and disclosure have their express Election Day restrictions, including the later-of-polls-close-or-receipt-deadline limit. Its recess/resumption provisions include an 11 PM election-night recess and subsequent-day work; they do not promise an exact release cadence or remaining eligible votes. Full method scopes can overlap state/native/early/absentee/privacy aggregates and are never indiscriminately summed.

Public form request bodies and redirect entities also survive. Earlier redirects lack separately recorded redirected-request headers; that missing metadata is explicit, not backfilled. Source publisher times/timezones and HTTP Date/Last-Modified remain different fields. All stored artifacts are strictly below 40,000,000 bytes; no original was truncated and no exact-byte split was needed. [Acquisition/schema details](data/results-sources/SCHEMA.md).

`run_offline.py` invokes only owned independent replay/extraction/validation scripts and read-only original references. It does not import producer calculators, mutable aggregate writers, optional coordinator reviews, a peer build or an acquisition/network function. Ledger processing uses one complete source body plus one ledger line at a time. Exact captured historical bodies resolve by old path **and old expected hash**. Full JSON/XML/CSV/XLSX/HTML structure retention preserves unrelated source content without making unrelated offices/demographics/media into further assignments. [Reproduction](data/results-sources/REPRODUCE.md).
