                  VOTER REGISTRATION
d Registration Requirements
Any person may register to vote, provided that they:
   are a citizen of the United States;
   live in Alabama;
   are at least 18 years old on or before election day;
   are not barred from voting due to a disqualifying felony conviction; and
   have not been declared mentally incompetent by a court.
Applicants may register the same day that they move into the state. Victims of
domestic violence, guardians of victims of domestic violence, federal and state
prosecutors, federal, state, probate and municipal judges, law enforcement
officers, legislators, and spouses of any of the aforementioned are entitled to
special legal protections related to personal identifying information. For further
information, contact the Secretary of State or your county Board of Registrars.
d When to Register
Voter registration is open all year, except during the 14 days prior to an election
and on Election Day. Voter registration is closed to provide enough time for
election officials to compile an accurate voter list for the election. If registering
to vote using a mail-in form, the registration form must be postmarked no later
than the registration deadline for an election. If registering to vote in person,
registration must be received before the close of business of the registration
deadline for an election. If registering to vote electronically, registration must
be submitted before 11:59 PM on the registration deadline for an election.

d Keeping Your Registration Updated
Any voter whose name or address is not correct on the voter list must provide
current information before being allowed to vote. To update voter registration
due to a change of name or address, voters will need to submit a new voter
registration form (see page 4) prior to the voter registration deadline. Please
note that the address on your Alabama Driver’s License does not need to match
your voter registration.

d Restoration of Voting Rights
An individual who is barred from registering and voting by reason of a
disqualifying felony conviction may request to have their voting rights restored
by applying for a Certificate of Eligibility to Register to Vote (CERV). An individual
may qualify for a Certificate of Eligibility to Register to Vote if they have fulfilled
all terms of the sentence for the disqualifying felony conviction. For further
information, please contact the Alabama Bureau of Pardons and Paroles at
334-353-7771 or visit pardons.state.al.us.

d Political Party Registration
Alabama does not have a party registration law; therefore, a citizen does not
declare a political party preference when registering to vote. When participating
in a primary election, a voter will be asked which party’s ballot they would like
to vote on upon checking in with a poll worker at the polls, or by indicating
party preference on an absentee ballot application.

                AT THE POLLING PLACE
d My Polling Place
Upon registering to vote or updating registration information, the county Board
of Registrars office sends a Voter ID card to each voter. The Voter ID card is a
postcard which includes the voter’s precinct information and polling location.
If you have not received a Voter ID card, contact your county Board of Registrars
office (see pages 18-19). Additionally, voters can verify their polling place on the
Secretary of State’s website by selecting My Voting Information. For municipal
election polling place information, contact the city clerk’s office.
d Polling Place Hours
Alabama polling places are open from 7 A.M. to 7 P.M. on Election Day. Please
note that polling places located in the Eastern Time Zone may be open from
7 A.M. to 7 P.M. Eastern Time. Anyone who attempts to join the voting line after
7:00 P.M. shall not be permitted to vote.
d Voting Procedure
1. The voter arrives at the polling place and provides their name to a
     poll worker. The voter must also present an acceptable form of photo
     identification (see page 6).
2. A poll worker checks the list of registered voters for the voter’s name. If
     the voter is listed as inactive, they will be asked to update their voter
     registration information by completing a Voter Update Form, available at
     the polling place, prior to being allowed to vote.
3. The voter signs the poll list. If participating in a Primary Election, the voter
     must declare which party’s primary they wish to vote in and then must sign
     that party’s poll list. If the election is a Primary Runoff, the voter must vote
     in the runoff of the same political party they chose for the Primary Election
     (see page 2 for crossover voting rules).
4. The poll worker will hand the voter a blank ballot. Then, the voter will be
     directed to a location designated by the polling place to fill out their ballot.
5. Upon filling out the ballot with the voter’s preferences, the voter will be
     directed to a secure ballot tabulator. The voter will insert their marked
     ballot into the secure ballot tabulator. If needed, the voter may request a
     poll worker for assistance with the secure ballot tabulator.
6. The secure ballot tabulator will display a message onscreen verifying the
     ballot has been counted.
 VOTING PROCEDURES VARY according to the type of equipment used and may
 vary by county. For more specific information, look for instructions printed
 in large print that should be visible at each polling place. Poll workers will
 provide information on voting procedures at the voter’s request. Poll workers
 are forbidden to tell a citizen for whom to vote for or attempt to influence a
 voter’s choices in any way.                  5
REPLACEMENT BALLOTS are available if the voter makes a mistake in marking
their ballot or if the ballot gets torn or otherwise damaged. The voter should
take the original ballot to a poll worker and request a replacement ballot. The
poll worker will issue a new ballot and place the original ballot in an envelope
marked “spoiled ballots” for security purposes.
PROHIBITIONS: Act 2021-535 prohibits any voting machine from being installed
or operated outside of the interior of the enclosed building designated as a voting
place. Additionally, no ballots shall be taken into or out of any voting place,
except when performing the authorized pre-election and post-election duties of
transporting ballots. Act 2021-488 prohibits any voter in this state from voting
more than once, or attempting to vote more than once, in any election held in
this state, or from voting in both this state and another state or territory in the
same or equivalent election. The act also prohibits any voter from attempting
to vote when they have knowledge that they are not entitled to vote, or the
commission of any kind of unlawful or fraudulent voting.

d If a Voter’s Name is Not on the Voter List
If a person’s name does not appear on the list of registered voters for the polling
place, the poll worker will contact the county Board of Registrars to determine if
the person is eligible to vote at that polling place. If the Board of Registrars are
unable to verify the person’s voter registration, the voter may vote a provisional
ballot.

d If a Voter does not have a Valid Photo ID
Prior to the date of the election, any registered voter who does not possess a
form of valid photo identification may receive a free Alabama Photo Voter ID
from their county Board of Registrars office or by requesting a homevisit by
calling 1 (800) 274-VOTE (8683).
A voter who does not have a valid form of photo ID on Election Day can vote
a regular ballot only if their name appears on the official voters list and they
are positively identified by at least two election officials. If the voter does not
have a valid form of photo ID and cannot be positively identified by at least two
election officials, they are permitted to vote a provisional ballot (see below for
additional information on voting a provisional ballot).

d Exemptions from Photo Voter ID Law
A VOTER WHO IS A MEMBER OF THE MILITARY OR RESIDING OVERSEAS —
Voters who vote by absentee ballot pursuant to the Uniformed and Overseas
Citizens Absentee Voting Act (UOCAVA) are not required to provide ID. The term
“UOCAVA voter” includes anyone who is a member of the Uniformed Services
(including their spouse and dependents), Merchant Marines, commissioned
corps of the Public Health Service, Commissioned Corps of the National Oceanic
and Atmospheric Administration, and any U.S. citizen residing overseas whose
last place of residency prior to moving overseas was within Alabama.
ELDERLY VOTERS AND VOTERS WITH A DISABILITY — Pursuant to the Voting
Accessibility for the Elderly and Handicapped Act, elderly voters at least 65
years of age and voters with disabilities are not required to provide ID when
voting by absentee ballot. Voters who fall under this category may select the
option on their absentee ballot application stating that they are physically
incapacitated and will not be able to vote in person on election day [ID Not
Required].

d Voting a Provisional Ballot
A voter using a provisional ballot to vote will follow the same procedures as
casting a regular ballot. Prior to receiving a provisional ballot, a voter will first
need to sign an affidavit attesting to their eligibility to vote and complete a
voter update form. Provisional ballots are placed in a separate secure ballot
box from regular ballots.
Reasons for Voting a Provisional Ballot:
Voter Name Not on Poll List - The provisional ballot will be counted if the
county Board of Registrars are able to confirm that the person casting the ballot
is a duly qualified elector of the polling place where the ballot was cast.
Absentee Ballot Never Received - A voter may cast a provisional ballot if they
did not receive the requested absentee ballot, OR if the voter did not return the
absentee ballot.
                                           7
Reasons for Voting a Provisional Ballot (cont.):
No Photo ID at Polling Place and Unable to be Positively Identified by Two
Election Officials - The voter has until 5 P.M. on the Friday following the election
to submit a valid form of photo ID to the Board of Registrars. If proper ID is sub-
mitted by this deadline, the ballot will be counted as long as all other require-
ments were met.
No Photo ID Included with Absentee Ballot Application (Non-exempt Voter) -
The voter has until 5 P.M. on the Friday following the election to submit a valid
form of photo ID to the Board of Registrars. If proper ID is submitted by this
deadline, the ballot will be counted as long as all other requirements were met.
Voter Objects to Political Party Indicated on Voter List (Applies only to
Primary Runoff Elections) - The provisional ballot will be counted only if the
Board of Registrars can confirm that the provisional ballot from the Primary
Runoff Election is of the same political party as the voter’s Primary Election
ballot.
d Sample Ballots
Voters may take marked sample ballots into the polling place, provided these
items are for personal use only and are not distruptive to others in the polling
place. The voter should not leave these items in the polling place.

d Campaigning on Election Day
Any Election Day campaigning, including the placing of signage, must maintain
a minimum distance of 30 feet from any polling place. If the polling location
is within a building, campaigning must not be closer than 30 feet from the
entrance of the building.

                    VOTER ASSISTANCE
Any voter who requests voting assistance may receive help from any person the
voter chooses except the voter’s employer, an agent of the employer, or an officer
or agent of the voter’s union. If the voter does not request a specific individual, a
poll worker may assist the voter at the request of the voter. Voters do not need to
state a reason for requesting assistance.
d Polling Place Accessability
Polling places should be accessible to people with disabilities. At every polling
place, there will be voting equipment to accommodate voters with disabilities
so that they can vote without assistance. If your specific disability is not
accommodated, please contact your county’s Probate Judge or the Secretary
of State.
d Provision for Elderly/Disabled Voters
Upon request from the voter, a polling official may allow a voter who is
physically disabled or over the age of 70 to move to the front of the line at a
polling place.

d Time in the Voting Booth
Poll workers should make every effort to give each voter adequate time to vote.
Any voter remaining in a voting station for four (4) minutes or longer may be
asked by a poll worker if he or she requires assistance.
IF THE VOTER DOES NOT WANT ASSISTANCE, they are permitted to remain for
one (1) additional minute. At the end of that minute, if individuals are waiting
to vote, the voter may be informed that time is up and may be asked to leave. If
there is no line, the voter may have as much time as necessary to finish voting.
IF THE VOTER WANTS ASSISTANCE, they may have at least five (5) additional
minutes to vote. At the end of the time, if others are waiting to vote, the assisted
voter may be told that time is up and may be asked to leave the polling place.
However, if there are no voters waiting, the assisted voter shall have as much
time as necessary to finish voting.

                    ELECTION PROCESS
The laws governing each type of election vary depending on the election.
Elections can generally be classified as either a Primary Election, a Primary
Runoff Election, a General Election, or a Special Election.

d Types of Elections
PRIMARY ELECTION — Held by political parties to select each party’s nominee
for the General Election. In Primary Elections, each party has a different ballot
style. Voters must choose to participate in either the Democratic Party Primary
or the Republican Party Primary (see crossover voting on page 2).
PRIMARY RUNOFF ELECTION - Occurs only if no one candidate receives a
majority of the votes cast in an election with at least three (3) candidates. The
Primary Runoff Election is held between the two candidates who receive the
highest number of votes in the first Primary. The winner of this Runoff Election
will be declared the party’s nominee for the General Election.
GENERAL ELECTION — Held to determine which candidate, regardless of
political party or write-in status, will be elected to a particular office up for
election. When voting in a General Election, voters have the option to either
vote a straight-party ticket or individually mark their candidates of preference.
SPECIAL ELECTION — Held to fill a vacancy in certain offices, such as U.S.
Representative, State Senator, or State Representative. Special Elections may
also be held to place specific issues before the voters, such as whether or not a
county or city will raise certain taxes or a Constitutional Amendment Election.

d Business/Medical Emergency Voting
Applications for business and medical emergency voting can be made after the
absentee deadline. However, the emergency absentee ballot application and
the voted absentee ballot must be returned no later than noon on the day the
election is held.
On the emergency absentee ballot application, the voter may designate
someone to turn in the application, receive the absentee ballot, and return the
voted absentee ballot to the Absentee Election Manager on behalf of the voter.
A voter may cast an emergency absentee ballot if they:
•      have a medical emergency requiring treatment from a licensed physician within
       five (5) days of an election
•      are required by an employer due to unforeseen circumstances within five (5) days
       before an election to be unavailable at the polls on Election Day
•      are a caregiver of a person who requires emergency treatment by a licensed phy-
       sician within five (5) days before an election
•      have a family member to the second degree of kinship by affinity or
       consanguinity (voter or voter’s spouse’s parents, grandparents, siblings, children
       or grandchildren) pass away within five (5) days before an election
In addition to application information outlined on page 12, the emergency application contains
an affidavit acknowledging that the voter was not aware of the circumstances prior to the
normal absentee ballot deadline. The application for medical emergency requires that the
attending physician describe and certify the circumstances as constituting an emergency.

d Military and Citizens Residing Overseas
Alabama citizens who are members of the Uniformed Services, spouses
and dependents of members of the Uniformed Services, Merchant Marines,
Commissioned Corps of the Public Health Service, Commissioned Corps of
the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, and Alabama overseas
citizens (also known as UOCAVA voters) have two methods for receiving their
absentee ballots: by electronic transmission or by U.S. Mail.
To request electronic transmission of the blank ballot, UOCAVA voters must
mark the appropriate box and provide an email address on the State of Alabama
“UOCAVA Application for Absentee Ballot” available from the Secretary of State’s
website, www.alabamavotes.gov. Additionally, UOCAVA voters may provide an
email address on the Federal Post Card Application available from their Voting
Assistance Officer or from the Federal Voting Assistance Program (FVAP) website,
www.fvap.gov, and selecting the “email/online” option for ballot transmittal.
UOCAVA voters may also return their absentee ballot by U.S. Mail or by a
commerical carrier. Please note that if returning the ballot by commercial air or
ground carrier, the date the ballot is tendered to the carrier is considered the
postmark date. UOCAVA voters should ensure that their ballot is postmarked or
tendered to the commercial carrier no later than the day of the election in which
they are casting a ballot.
UOCAVA voters are no longer required to have their ballot’s affidavit envelope
notarized. However, the affidavit on the envelope must be witnessed by two
persons 18 years of age or older.
UOCAVA voters may find additional information on absentee voting on the
Federal Voting Assistance Program (FVAP) website at fvap.gov. Please note that
the FVAP website provides information for UOCAVA voters only.


dCitizens with Permanent Disabilities
Citizens who have a permanent disability which prevents them from attending
the polls may apply for an absentee ballot on an annual basis. The application
is valid for all federal, state, county, and municipal elections in the calendar
year and must be renewed annually. A voter who is permanently disabled must
provide verification from their primary physician. The application must be
signed and notarized by the voter’s primary physician.
Voters submitting an absentee application on the basis of being permanently
disabled must submit their application to their county Absentee Election
Manager. For absentee application deadlines for the 2026 Election, see page 13.

d Absentee Ballot Application
To apply for an absentee ballot, submit an Absentee Ballot Application
(available for download at www.alabamavotes.gov) to your county Absentee
Election Manager. Applications may also be requested online or by calling the
Elections Division at (334) 242-7210.
Alternatively, voters may request an absentee ballot by writing or visiting their
county Absentee Election Manager and providing the following information:
y name and residential address
y a copy of valid photo identification (see page 6)
y election for which the ballot is requested
y reason for absence from the polls on Election Day
y party choice, if the election is a Primary or Primary Runoff**
y address to which the ballot should be mailed
y voter’s signature (if signed with a mark, it must be witnessed)
  **It is not necessary to give party choice when applying for an absentee ballot for a General
  Election. However, in a Primary Election, a voter may participate in only one political party’s
  primary; therefore, a choice must be designated on the application so the appropriate ballot
   can be provided. If the voter declines to designate a party choice for a Primary or Primary
   Runoff Election, the Absentee Election Manager may send only the ballot for constitutional
                                           amendments.
The absentee ballot application must be returned to the Absentee Election
Manager by U.S. Mail or by commercial carrier no later than the seventh day
prior to the election; or returned in person by the voter no later than the fifth
day prior to the election.
Upon receiving the absentee ballot application, the Absentee Election Manager
may request additional evidence on the reason for voting absentee if the voter
has a history of continuous absentee voting.
APPLICATIONS FOR MILITARY PERSONNEL AND OVERSEAS CITIZENS —
Applications submitted by military personnel, their spouses or dependents,
and U.S. citizens residing overseas are valid for all federal, state, and county
elections in the current calendar year. However, these voters should ensure
that they keep their mailing and email addresses updated with their county
Board of Registrars and with their Absentee Election Manager.
APPLICATIONS FOR CITIZENS WITH PERMANENT DISABILITIES — Applications
submitted by citizens who are permanently disabled and cannot attend the
polls are valid for all federal, state, county, and municipal elections in the
current calendar year. However, these voters should ensure that they keep
their mailing addresses updated with their county Board of Registrars and with
their Absentee Election Manager.

BALLOT PROCEDURE — The absentee ballot package will contain three
envelopes —the secrecy envelope, the affidavit envelope, and one plain, pre-
addressed envelope (the outer envelope). Once the voter completely fills out
their ballot, they’ll follow the below procedure for preparing the ballot for
return:
   Seal the ballot in the plain envelope (the secrecy envelope)
   Place the plain envelope inside the affidavit envelope
   Seal the affidavit envelope and completely fill out the affidavit outside
    of the envelope
   Sign the affidavit and have the signature witnessed by either a notary
    public or two witnesses 18 years of age or older
   Place the affidavit envelope inside the outer envelope
   NOTE: If you are voting a provisional absentee ballot due to lack of
    identification, place a copy of your ID (not the original) inside the outer
    envelope.
WITNESSES OR NOTARIZATION — An absentee ballot cannot be counted
unless the affidavit is notarized or has the signatures of two witnesses who
are 18 years old or older. Electronic or remote notarization of absentee ballot
affidavits is not allowed.
SUBMITTING THE VOTED BALLOT — The voter is required to use one of the
following methods for returning their absentee ballot:
   send the ballot to the Absentee Election Manager by U.S. Mail, by
    commercial carrier; or
   personally hand-deliver the ballot to the Absentee Election Manager (or
    by a designee in the case of medical emergency voting).
VOTING DEADLINE — An absentee ballot returned by hand must be delivered
by the voter and must be in the Absentee Election Manager’s office by the
close of business (but no later than 5 P.M.) on the day prior to the election. An
absentee ballot returned by mail must be received by the Absentee Election
Manager no later than noon on Election Day.

                                  FAQs
z I TURNED IN A VOTER REGISTRATION APPLICATION. AM I REGISTERED
  TO VOTE? No. Your voter registration application must first be approved by
  the county Board of Registrars. Once your application has been approved,
  you should receive a notification from the Registrars indicating the status
  of your application. This notification will typically be a voter identification
  postcard confirming that you are registered to vote. However, if your
  application was incomplete, you may receive a letter requesting additional
  information to complete your application. If you are unsure about the
  status of your application, you should call your local Board of Registrars or
  visit www.alabamavotes.gov.
z I TURN 18 AFTER THE VOTER REGISTRATION DEADLINE BUT BEFORE THE
  ELECTION. CAN I VOTE IN THAT ELECTION? Yes. Voters who will be 18
  years of age on or before Election Day may submit a voter registration form
  prior to turning 18. Please note that the voter registration application must
  be received prior to the voter registration blackout, which is within 14 days
  of any election.
z I HAVE MOVED FROM ONE PART OF THE COUNTY TO ANOTHER. CAN I
  STILL VOTE AT MY OLD POLLING PLACE? No. You must update your voter
  registration by either reregistering to vote online or by mailing in a new
  voter registration form to the Board of Registrars in your county. If you have
  moved within the county and have not updated your voter registration
  within 14 days of an election, contact your county Board of Registrars
  office for your new polling place. You will be permitted to go to your new
  polling place, update your voter registration, and cast a provisional ballot.
z I HAVE MOVED FROM MY COUNTY OF RESIDENCE. CAN I STILL VOTE AT
  MY OLD POLLING PLACE? No. By signing in at your old polling place, you
  are falsley attesting that you still live at your old address. Any changes
  to a voter’s registration information must be made within 14 days of any
  election.
z WHAT IF I MAKE A MISTAKE ON MY BALLOT? If you have not already placed
  your ballot in the secure ballot tabulator, you may ask a poll worker
  for another ballot. The poll worker will place your spoiled ballot in a
  designated secure area so that it cannot be counted or reused.
z IS THERE A PROCEDURE FOR WHEN VOTING EQUIPMENT FAILS? Yes. There
  are procedures poll workers will follow in the event the voting equipment
  is experiencing techical difficulties. If the ballot tabulator is down, it
  is standard to have voters place their ballots in a secure bin below the
  tabulator. The ballots will be inserted into the tabulator by a poll worker at
  the close of polls.
                                     15
z ARE CANDIDATES ALLOWED TO CAMPAIGN OUTSIDE MY POLLING PLACE?
  Campaigning outside the polling place is permitted; however, this activity
  must not be closer than 30 feet from the entrance of the polling place. If the
  polling place is located in a room within a building, the campaigning must
  not be closer than 30 feet from the entrance of the building. If a candidate
  or their supporters are campaigning within 30 feet of the entrance of the
  polling place, notify the chief inspector of the polling place.
z CAN A CANDIDATE ASSIST ME IN MARKING MY BALLOT? Yes, if you
  specifically ask the candidate to help you. The only people who cannot
  assist a voter are the voter’s employer, an agent of the voter’s employer,
  or an officer or agent of the voter’s union.
z CAN I WEAR CAMPAIGN BUTTONS OR T-SHIRTS WITH POLITICAL
  ADVERTISEMENTS INTO THE POLLING PLACE? Yes. However, you should
  not loiter or leave any campaign materials in the polling place.
z IF CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENTS ARE INCLUDED ON THE BALLOT, MUST
  I VOTE ON THEM? No. You are not required to vote on constitutional
  amendments. Similarly, you are not required to vote in all races on the
  ballot. Participation is your choice.
z WHAT HAPPENS IF I DO NOT WANT TO DECLARE A POLITICAL PARTY
  PREFERENCE AT THE PRIMARY ELECTION? If you choose not to declare a
  political preference at the Primary Election, you will not be eligible to vote
  in any political party’s Primary Election. However, you are still eligible to
  vote on any proposed constitutional amendments that are up for a vote.
z I VOTED IN MY POLITICAL PARTY’S PRIMARY ELECTION. MY PARTY IS
  NOT HAVING A RUNOFF. CAN I VOTE IN THE OTHER PARTY’S RUNOFF
  ELECTION? No. Alabama has a cross over voting law that prohibits anyone
  who voted in one party’s Primary from voting in another party’s Primary
  Runoff. If you do not vote in the Primary Election, you may vote in any
  party’s Primary Runoff Election. See Page 2 for cross over voting rules.
z CAN I TAKE A CELL PHONE INTO MY POLLING PLACE? Yes. However, use of
  the phone in the polling place should not disturb other voters or disrupt
  the polling place.
z CAN I TAKE PHOTOGRAPHS INSIDE MY POLLING PLACE? Yes. However, an
  individual may only take a photograph of their own ballot. Taking other
  photographs within the polling place may intimidate other voters and is
  not allowed.
